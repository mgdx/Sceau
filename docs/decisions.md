# Decisions and deviations from SPEC.md

> Decisions D1 to D19 have been incorporated into SPEC.md since 2026-09-25.

`SPEC.md` is the source of truth. Any decision that departs from it, or that settles a point the SPEC leaves open, is recorded here with its rationale. One entry per decision, most recent at the bottom.

Format: date, context, decision, rationale, deviation from the SPEC concerned.

## D1. Embedded trust store: ANTS CSCAs and the German Master List from the BSI

- **Date**: 2026-09-25 (user's decision).
- **Context**: SPEC §7.1 plans to embed the ANTS CSCA certificates and the ICAO Master List (`icao-master-list.ml`), downloaded from the ICAO PKD website.
- **Decision**: v1 embeds in `core/src/main/resources/trust/`:
  - the 5 French CSCA certificates from ANTS: passport 2010, 2015, 2020, 2025, and e-ID 2021 (identity cards);
  - the German Master List published by the BSI (Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik), file `de-bsi-master-list.ml`, signed on 2026-05-28: 588 certificates, 112 issuers, covering the 27 EU countries, the EEA, Switzerland and the United Kingdom.

  The provenance, fingerprints and update procedure of each file are given in `docs/trust-store.md`.
- **Rationale**:
  - *Why not the ICAO Master List*: its terms of use forbid redistribution, and downloading it goes through a captcha (mandatory human interaction), which also prevents a tooled update.
  - *Why the BSI one*: among the public national Master Lists evaluated, the BSI one is published with explicit reuse terms, which allow reuse, including commercial reuse, subject to three conditions: no advertising use, no appearance of cooperation with or endorsement by the BSI, file redistributed unmodified. Sceau meets these three conditions: the file is embedded byte for byte (its fingerprint is checked by a test), and both the documentation and the publication metadata refer to it as the “German Master List from the BSI” without implying any partnership.
  - *Why keep the ANTS certificates*: the French e-ID CSCA (`CN=eID-FRANCE`), which signs the CNIe, does not appear in any national Master List, including the BSI one. Without the ANTS certificate, no CNIe could be verified.
  - *Anchoring the embedded list*: verifying the CMS signature of the Master List with the signer certificate it contains would be circular (anyone can sign a list with their own certificate). The signature is therefore verified **and** the signer certificate must be signed by the German CSCA whose SHA-256 fingerprint, published by the BSI, is pinned in the code (`2084aed7…ada8`, or its link certificate `5f28cb69…20bd`; full fingerprints in the code and in `docs/trust-store.md`). Otherwise the embedded list is rejected.
  - *User imports*: they follow the rule of SPEC §5.4: CMS signature verified with the signer certificate embedded in the file, whose fingerprint is shown to the user for confirmation before import.
- **Known limitations of the BSI list** (to be mentioned on the “About” screen along with the limitations of SPEC §7.4):
  - the CSCA for Slovak identity cards and 4 Hungarian CSCAs from 2024 are missing from it; they are present in the Italian and Swedish Master Lists, which the user can import;
  - the French e-ID CSCA is not in it (hence the ANTS certificates).
  A document whose issuer is covered neither by ANTS, nor by the BSI list, nor by an import gives “Unknown issuer” (SPEC §7.4).
- **Size**: the Master List adds about 0.5 MB compressed to the APK.
- **Tests**: the test “embedded file fingerprints match `docs/trust-store.md`” covers the 5 ANTS certificates and `de-bsi-master-list.ml`. The test “Master List with invalid CMS signature rejected” applies to loading the embedded list as well as to import.
- **Deviations from the SPEC**: §7.1 (BSI Master List instead of the ICAO one; file `de-bsi-master-list.ml` instead of `icao-master-list.ml`); §7.2 (additional anchoring of the embedded list's signer on a pinned German CSCA, stricter than the SPEC).

## D2. ABI splits: one APK per architecture, plus a universal APK

- **Date**: 2026-09-25 (revised the same day following decision D3).
- **Context**: SPEC §2 and §10 call for per-architecture APKs, each under 8 MB. A first version of this decision kept a single universal APK, as long as the application remained pure Java/Kotlin.
- **Decision**: ABI splits are enabled in `app/build.gradle.kts` for `armeabi-v7a`, `arm64-v8a`, `x86` and `x86_64`, plus a universal APK (`isUniversalApk = true`).
- **Rationale**: the JPEG 2000 decoder is now native (OpenJPEG via JNI, decision D3). Each per-ABI APK embeds only the `libsceau_jp2.so` for its architecture (170 to 255 KB); the universal APK contains all of them, for channels that do not distinguish between architectures. Sizes of the unsigned release APKs, BSI Master List included: about 3.7 MB per ABI, 4.4 MB for the universal one, under the 8 MB target. The library is aligned on 16 KB pages (Android 15+), and stored uncompressed and aligned in the APK.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none: compliant with §2 (“APK per architecture”) and §10. The universal APK is an addition.

## D3. JPEG 2000 decoder: OpenJPEG via JNI, built from source

- **Date**: 2026-09-25 (user's decision).
- **Context**: SPEC §3 chooses jj2000 (JMRTD fork), “pure Java, no JNI”, and mentions openjpeg as a possible replacement. The original jj2000 license is not free: it states that “No license under any patent, copyright or other intellectual property rights is granted for non JPEG 2000 Standard conforming products”, a field-of-use restriction that Debian deemed non-free (the module was removed there for that reason). It is incompatible with Sceau's GPLv3 and with F-Droid's inclusion policy. Moreover, the JMRTD fork is not published on Maven Central.
- **Decision**: the JPEG 2000 portrait (DG2, and the images in DG12) is decoded by **OpenJPEG** (BSD-2-Clause), via JNI, in the `:app` module:
  - OpenJPEG sources as a git submodule `app/src/main/cpp/openjpeg`, pinned to commit `8314119b` of `master` (`v2.5.4-29`, 2026-09-05; see “Pinned version” below); only the `openjp2` library is built (without codecs, tools or tests), statically, then linked into `libsceau_jp2.so` by CMake via the NDK. No prebuilt binary in the repository;
  - Kotlin API `io.github.mgdx.sceau.jp2.Jpeg2000Decoder.decode(bytes): Bitmap?`; in-memory decoding core in `app/src/main/cpp/jp2_decode.c`, separate from the JNI bridge (`jp2_jni.c`) and tested on the host under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer (`app/src/main/cpp/test/run-host-tests.sh`);
  - `:core` stays pure Kotlin without JNI: the former `Images.decodeJpeg2000` is removed; `:core` passes on the portrait bytes (`EncodedImage`) and `:app` decodes them.
- **Rationale**: OpenJPEG is the reference implementation of JPEG 2000, under a free license compatible with GPLv3, maintained, and present in F-Droid as well as in distributions. Building it from source satisfies F-Droid's rebuildability requirement. Decoding potentially hostile data is bounded: dimensions limited to 4096 × 4096 (checked as soon as the header is read), memory budget and resolution reduction (see “Decompression bomb”), stream limited to 16 MB, 1 to 4 components of at most 16 bits, OpenJPEG strict mode (truncated stream rejected), silent message handlers (no trace, SPEC §8), intermediate native buffers (copy of the stream, decoded samples, ARGB pixels) zeroed before being freed. Hardened build: `-fstack-protector-strong`, `_FORTIFY_SOURCE=2`, `-fvisibility=hidden` (only the JNI function is exported), full RELRO.
- **OpenJPEG internal buffers** (code blocks, tiles being decoded, stream read buffer, copies of the compressed data): all their allocations go through `jp2_alloc.c`, which knows the size of each block and zeroes it before `free`, including the old block of a reallocation, via `sceau_jp2_secure_zero` (a write followed by a compiler barrier, which the optimizer cannot remove; `explicit_bzero` is not guaranteed by bionic for minSdk 26). **Remaining limitation**: pages returned to the system by the allocator and any copies made by the JVM (stream byte array, `Bitmap`) are not covered here; the latter are the result screen's responsibility.
- **Decompression bomb (audit V4, 2026-09-25)**: a J2K stream of a few kilobytes can make OpenJPEG allocate more than a gigabyte (4 × 4 code blocks: 1.2 GB; 4 × 4 precincts: 7 GB; 16,384 tiles: 450 MB), whereas DG2 and the DG12 images are decoded in the same process, on phones with 2 to 3 GB. Defense in three layers:
  - *estimate before `opj_decode`* (`jp2_decode.c`, after `opj_read_header`, based on `opj_get_cstr_info`): decoded samples (components × pixels at the chosen resolution × 4 bytes, since OpenJPEG stores each sample on 32 bits whatever its precision, itself bounded to 16 bits), samples of the current tile if the stream is tiled, parameters of each tile (8 KB + 1.1 KB per component), code blocks of all resolutions of a tile (448 bytes each + 16 per quality layer), sub-bands, copy of the compressed data, and a fixed 4 MB (codec, 1 MB read buffer, wavelets). Unit costs taken from OpenJPEG's structures on 64-bit and verified by measurement: the estimate exceeds the actual peak by 5 to 20% on legitimate 4096 × 4096 streams;
  - *reduce rather than reject*: the decoded image is capped at 2048 × 2048 (well beyond a portrait or a signature); the smallest resolution reduction (`opj_set_decoded_resolution_factor`, halving at each level) that fits both this cap **and** the budget is chosen. If none fits (too many code blocks or tiles, whose cost does not drop with resolution), the stream is rejected (`SCEAU_JP2_ERR_TOO_LARGE`) before any decoding. Announced dimensions remain bounded to 4096 × 4096;
  - *hard cap on allocation*: `opj_malloc.c` is removed from the `openjp2` target at CMake configuration time (the submodule is not modified) and replaced by `jp2_alloc.c`, which counts every OpenJPEG allocation (system allocator overhead included) and refuses those that would exceed the budget of the current decoding. It covers what the estimate does not see: tables allocated during `opj_read_header` (parameters of the 65,535 possible tiles), tile-specific parameters, JP2 palettes, and declared precincts (`opj_get_cstr_info` copies their sizes incorrectly, bytes instead of integers: they are not estimated).

  **Chosen budget**: 64 MB for OpenJPEG (`SCEAU_JP2_MEMORY_BUDGET`), plus the output pixels, converted line by line directly into the Java array (at most 2048 × 2048 × 4 = 16 MB) and then copied once into the `Bitmap` (16 MB): a decode stays under 96 MB all told. `Jpeg2000Decoder` serializes decodes (only one at a time in the process), so DG2 and DG12 no longer add up their peaks.

  **Measurements** (process `maxrss` on the host, measurement driver outside the repository; before: `v2.5.4` and the former `jp2_decode.c`): `bomb_cb4p` 1226 → 2 MB (rejected); `bomb_cb8` 565 → 2 MB (rejected); `bomb_tiles` 449 → 3 MB (rejected); `bomb_base` (4 components of 16 bits, 4096 × 4096) 328 → 29 MB (decoded at 1024 × 1024); 4 × 4 precincts 7069 → 70 MB (rejected by the allocation cap). Legitimate images: the harness images and `specimen-portrait.jp2` decode pixel for pixel as before; a legitimate 4096 × 4096 RGB image goes from 274 MB to 76 MB and comes out at 2048 × 2048. The harness (`run-host-tests.sh`) generates by script a large legitimate image and five hostile streams (4 × 4 blocks in J2K and JP2, 4 × 4 precincts, 16,384 tiles, 4 components of 16 bits) and checks that OpenJPEG's allocation peak never exceeds the budget.
- **Pinned version (audit V5, 2026-09-25)**: the tag `v2.5.4` (`6c4a29b0`) does not contain the security fixes published since then on `master`, and no 2.5.5 release has come out. The submodule is therefore advanced to `8314119b`, the latest commit of `origin/master` as of the audit date, which includes:
  - `91d08b11` (2026-02-10): heap overflow in `opj_j2k_read_sod` (tile-part index), reachable during decoding through a crafted stream;
  - `1bb9e915` (2026-06-10): interleaved tile parts, invalid index and read at the wrong position;
  - `91966314` (2026-09-04): MCO marker with no stage, level shift wrongly reset to zero;
  - outside decoding or without effect on Sceau: `839936aa` (integer overflow on the encoder side), NEON-optimized 5-3 and 9-7 IDWT (`0186472b`, `0fc6e243`, active on arm64-v8a and armeabi-v7a), removal of unused variables, `cmake_minimum_required` raised to 3.10, pkg-config generation, CI and README.

  Review of the `v2.5.4..8314119b` log: no change to `openjpeg.h` or to the API used by `jp2_decode.c`, no new dependency, no binary file (only C, CMake, CI scripts and a C regression test). Host harness rerun under ASan and UBSan: green. The NEON IDWTs only run on ARM and remain to be validated on a device (decoding of the reference portrait).
- **Monitoring (unmaintained project)**: since `06bbae8b` (2026-07-07), the upstream README declares the repository “unmaintained”: commits may still arrive, without regular triage of issues. At each Sceau release: reread `git log` of `origin/master` since `8314119b` and the security advisories targeting OpenJPEG (NVD, OSS-Fuzz, Debian `openjpeg2` tracker), advance the submodule if a fix affects decoding, and rerun `run-host-tests.sh`. Fallback plan if a vulnerability is no longer fixed upstream: apply the fix in a fork of the submodule published under the project's account (sources only, F-Droid requirement unchanged), or follow the branch maintained by a distribution (Debian, Fedora). Sceau's own defenses (strict mode, size bounds, memory budget and allocation cap, harness under sanitizers) limit exposure in the meantime. Any advance of the submodule must also check that `opj_malloc.c` still exposes the same functions (otherwise `jp2_alloc.c` must be adapted: linking fails) and that the estimate's unit costs remain valid (`big.j2k` and the harness bombs).
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §3 (OpenJPEG instead of jj2000; native code via JNI, whereas the SPEC asked for “no JNI”). Consequence: ABI splits enabled (D2).

## D4. Package name `io.github.mgdx.sceau`

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: `CLAUDE.md` cited the package `fr.sceau.core` as an example.
- **Decision**: application ID and package `io.github.mgdx.sceau` for `:app`, `io.github.mgdx.sceau.core` for `:core`.
- **Rationale**: a package identifier must correspond to a domain one controls. The project does not own `sceau.fr`; the domain `mgdx.github.io` is tied to the account hosting the repository, which is also the convention recognized by F-Droid.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none (the SPEC does not set the package).

## D5. Strings split by screen

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §2 puts all strings in `res/values/strings.xml`.
- **Decision**: strings are split into one file per screen (`strings_home.xml`, `strings_reading.xml`, `strings_result.xml`, `strings_trust.xml`, `strings_about.xml`), plus `strings.xml` for shared strings, in `res/values/` and `res/values-en/`.
- **Rationale**: several developers work in parallel on different screens; a single file would be a constant source of merge conflicts. For Android, all files in `res/values*/` form a single resource table: the spirit of the rule (no hardcoded string, translations ready, `values-en` provided) is respected.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §2 (letter of the rule “in `res/values/strings.xml`”).

## D6. “Not available” status for the certificate chain and DS validity

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §5.3 distinguishes “Unknown issuer” from “Failed”, and SPEC §6.1 allows an “estimated” issue date (expiry minus the usual validity period) when neither DG12 nor the SOD signing date provides it. But the usual period is not always the right one: a minor's passport is valid for 5 years instead of 10.
- **Decision**:
  - when no CSCA in the store matches the DS, the “Certificate chain” line (`CERTIFICATE_CHAIN`) is `NOT_AVAILABLE`, not `FAILED`; this status is what produces the “Unknown issuer” verdict;
  - the “DS certificate validity” line (`DS_VALIDITY`) is never `FAILED` on an **estimated** issue date: it is then `NOT_AVAILABLE`, with the detail stating that the date is estimated.
  - The verdict is computed from the decision table in `docs/protocol.md`: a `FAILED` or `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM` line gives “Failed”; otherwise a `NOT_AVAILABLE` chain gives “Unknown issuer”; otherwise successful PA and successful CA or AA give “Authentic”; CA and AA not available give “Valid signature, chip not verified”; any other case gives “Failed”.
- **Rationale**: an issuer missing from the store is not proof of forgery (SPEC §7.4); an estimate cannot justify a failure, at the risk of declaring an authentic minor's passport fake.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none in substance; clarifies §5.3 and §6.1.

## D7. Internal permission `DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION` (removed on 2026-10-06)

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §2 only allows `android.permission.NFC`. The merged manifest additionally contains `io.github.mgdx.sceau.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION`, added by androidx.core.
- **Decision**: the permission is kept.
- **Rationale**: it is a `signature`-level permission, declared and held by the application itself, which protects non-exported dynamic receivers; it is required by `ContextCompat.registerReceiver` below API 33. It grants no access to the network or to any user data, and is not shown to the user.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §2 (letter of the rule “`android.permission.NFC` only”), without effect on its purpose.
- **Update of 2026-10-06**: since commit `f6cd14b`, Sceau no longer registers any broadcast receiver (`nfc/NfcStatus.kt` rereads the NFC state by polling, no more `registerReceiver` in the code). Yet the permission remains in the merged manifest: androidx.core declares it in its own manifest, whether or not the application uses it. Observed on the universal release APK of version 0.9 (`aapt2 dump permissions`: `uses-permission android.permission.NFC`, `permission` and `uses-permission io.github.mgdx.sceau.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION`, nothing else). It is therefore no longer needed by Sceau; removing it (`tools:node="remove"` in `AndroidManifest.xml`) is possible but is not done here: an AndroidX dependency that called `ContextCompat.registerReceiver` below API 33 would then fail at runtime. Decision left to the author, the rationale above (`signature` permission, no access) remaining valid.
- **Revision of 2026-10-06**: decision reversed, the permission is removed from the merged manifest (`tools:node="remove"` on the `<permission>` and the `<uses-permission>` in `AndroidManifest.xml`). No class in the release APK calls `registerReceiver` (strings of the dex of the universal 0.9 APK): the risk described above does not apply. The manifest now requests only `android.permission.NFC`, in line with the letter of SPEC §2. To be rechecked at each AndroidX upgrade (a dependency that started calling `ContextCompat.registerReceiver` below API 33 would throw a `SecurityException`) and on an Android 8 to 12 device (TP-01).
- **Revision of 2026-10-07**: same approach extended to unnecessary AndroidX components (profileinstaller's exported receiver, profileinstaller and emoji2 initializers): see D46.

## D8. Lint rules disabled or ignored

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §2 requires zero lint warnings, as errors in CI.
- **Decision**:
  - rules `AndroidGradlePluginVersion`, `GradleDependency` and `NewerVersionAvailable` disabled in `app/build.gradle.kts` and `core/build.gradle.kts`;
  - rule `TrustAllX509TrustManager` ignored for the `bcpkix-jdk18on` jar only (`app/lint.xml`).
- **Rationale**:
  - the first three report that a newer version exists: they query the network and broke CI at every upstream release, unrelated to the code. Version upgrades are made deliberately;
  - `TrustAllX509TrustManager` targets a class in the `org.bouncycastle.est` package (EST protocol, RFC 7030) that Sceau does not use, that can do nothing without network permission, and that R8 removes in release. The exclusion applies to this jar only: the rule stays active for the project's code.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none (zero warnings maintained).

## D9. `:core` dependencies as `implementation`

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: `:app` depends on `:core`, which depends on JMRTD, SCUBA, BouncyCastle and kotlinx-coroutines.
- **Decision**: these dependencies are declared as `implementation` in `core/build.gradle.kts`, not as `api`.
- **Rationale**: the public API of `:core` (`readAndVerify`, `CardTransport`, `AccessKey`, `VerificationReport`, `TrustStore`…) exposes no JMRTD or BouncyCastle type, only Kotlin and `java.security` / `java.time` types. `:app` must not depend on the implementation details of `:core`. Only `scuba-sc-android`, needed for the `IsoDep` bridge, is declared directly in `:app`.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none.

## D10. Passport dates entered with the keyboard

- **Date**: 2026-09-25 (user feedback on a phone).
- **Context**: SPEC §5.1 plans a date picker for the date of birth and the expiry date (Passport tab). In testing, the picker opened as a calendar, impractical for copying a date of birth or a distant expiry date.
- **Decision**: both dates are entered with the numeric keyboard (`KeyboardType.NumberPassword`), as eight digits (day and month on two, year on four). The `/` separators are displayed as the user types (`DateDigitsTransformation`) without being stored. The order of the fields follows the locale's short date format (`DateOrder.forLocale`): DD/MM/YYYY in French, MM/DD/YYYY in US English; day, month, year by default. Validation (`AccessForm`): real date, birth no later than today, expiry from 1990 onward; the error is shown under the field and “Read” stays disabled as long as a date is invalid. The digits live in the `SessionViewModel`, never in a `Bundle`.
- **Rationale**: faster entry than the calendar for dates copied from the document; the numeric password keyboard prevents the system keyboard from learning or suggesting these dates.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.1 (formatted numeric fields instead of a date picker).

## D11. Technical code shown for all reading errors

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §5.2 plans a technical identifier shown “in small print” for the unexpected error only. On a real device, errors presented as “timed out” or “document removed” had other causes; yet no log is allowed (SPEC §8), in debug as in release: the displayed code is the only means of diagnosis.
- **Decision**: the reading screen shows, under the French message, the full code of the `SceauException` for **all** errors. The message is chosen from the base code (`ReadingErrors.messageFor`, text before the first `-`). The codes `TIMEOUT`, `CONNECTION_LOST` and `UNEXPECTED` carry a diagnostic suffix made solely of elements without personal data: step (`SECURE_CHANNEL`…), INS byte of the current command (`INS86`), APDU length rounded to the nearest ten (`L10`), I/O error subtype taken from a closed list of `android.nfc` messages (`IO-TRANSCEIVE_FAILED`, `IO-TOO_LONG`, `IO-SERVICE_DIED`, otherwise `IO-OTHER` or `IO-NO_MESSAGE`; the raw message is never reused), exception class and SW status word. Example: `TIMEOUT-SECURE_CHANNEL-INS86-L10`. Full list in `docs/protocol.md` §5.
- **Rationale**: diagnose on a device without writing anything to the logs; the suffix contains no data read, no key, and no APDU bytes.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.2 (technical identifier shown for all errors, not just the unexpected error).

## D12. 60 s response timeout during authentication

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: a real French passport, after several failed attempts, stayed silent on the first authentication command (first GENERAL AUTHENTICATE of PACE, then EXTERNAL AUTHENTICATE of BAC) on Fairphone 3 and Fairphone 5: reading failed with a timeout (`TIMEOUT-SECURE_CHANNEL-INS82-L50`). Without a short limit, it took 23.3 s to answer the first GENERAL AUTHENTICATE of PACE: this is the chips' anti-brute-force countermeasure, which imposes an increasing delay after failed attempts.
- **Decision**:
  - during PACE and BAC, the transport's response timeout is raised to 60 s (`SecureChannel.AUTHENTICATION_TIMEOUT_MILLIS`), then restored to its previous value (10 s, `IsoDepTransport.DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MILLIS`) for reading;
  - `IsoDepTransport` rereads `IsoDep.getTimeout()` after each assignment and classifies a failure as a timeout based on the timeout actually applied by the system (failure occurring after 90% of that timeout);
  - after 5 s in the “Opening secure channel” step, the reading screen shows a waiting message (the chip makes you wait, up to a minute); the “Incorrect CAN or MRZ” message warns that a failed attempt can lengthen this delay;
  - a timeout during PACE stops reading (`TIMEOUT-…`), without falling back to BAC (decision D13).
- **Rationale**: cutting off earlier never lets the chip answer, and each interrupted attempt can worsen the penalty. Restarting BAC on a chip under penalty would add an interrupted attempt.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none; clarifies §5.2 (“Timed out”) and §6.1 step 2.

## D13. Fallback from PACE to BAC for an MRZ key

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §6.1 step 2 plans PACE if EF.CardAccess announces it, BAC otherwise. A real French passport (ISO 14443-B, Fairphone 3) announced PACE but did not answer its first GENERAL AUTHENTICATE, then lost the link. ICAO 9303 still requires documents announcing PACE to accept BAC during the transition period.
- **Decision**: with an MRZ key, if PACE fails other than through a key rejection (SW `63xx`) or a timeout, that is, on an unexpected SW, a JMRTD error or a lost link, the link is reset by `CardTransport.reconnect()` (method added to the interface, empty by default; `IsoDepTransport` implements it with `close()` then `connect()`, timeout reapplied), the ICAO applet is selected in the clear, then BAC is performed. With a CAN, no fallback: BAC cannot use a CAN, the error is returned as is (PACE rejection or failure: `ACCESS_DENIED`). A failed reconnection gives `CONNECTION_LOST-SECURE_CHANNEL-RECONNECT`. Tests: `PaceFallbackTest`.
- **Rationale**: read documents whose PACE implementation is faulty, without multiplying key attempts (an explicit rejection stops everything) or restarting a chip under penalty (D12).
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 2 (BAC attempted after an announced PACE fails); §6 (`CardTransport` gains a method, `readAndVerify` unchanged).

## D14. Chip reading: short APDUs, optional DG2, JMRTD logs and exceptions

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: implementation points of reading (`core/src/main/kotlin/…/reading/`) that the SPEC does not settle, or that it only allows to be met in part.
- **Decision**:
  - **Short APDUs**: the maximum length passed to JMRTD is the transport's, capped at 256 (`Chip`); no extended APDU is sent, as many chips refuse them. Reading in blocks of 223 bytes, without SFI (SELECT then READ BINARY), response MACs always verified;
  - **Missing DG2 tolerated**: DG2 is always requested, but a chip that does not provide it does not interrupt reading: the photo is absent and the “Data group hashes” line covers only the DGs read. DG1 and EF.SOD remain mandatory; EF.COM is optional (the SOD then indicates the DGs present);
  - **JMRTD and SCUBA logs switched off**: their `java.util.logging` loggers (`org.jmrtd`, `net.sf.scuba`) are set to level `OFF` at the start of each read (`LibraryLogging`), because they write APDUs in the clear;
  - **Exceptions without attached cause**: JMRTD exception messages contain APDUs in hexadecimal, hence potentially data read. No JMRTD exception is propagated or attached as the cause of a `SceauException`; only a technical identifier is built (step, label, class, SW: `technicalCode`);
  - **Buffers that cannot be wiped**: JMRTD (file read streams, LDS objects, secure messaging) and OpenJPEG (decision D3) keep intermediate copies of the data in internal buffers that they free without zeroing. Sceau zeroes its own arrays (`DocumentData.wipe()`, native buffers of `jp2_decode.c`) but cannot reach those.
- **Rationale**: widest compatibility; SPEC §8 (no data read in a log or an exception). The buffer limitation stems from the chosen libraries (SPEC §3, D3); these copies stay in the process memory, and are neither written to disk nor logged.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 4 (missing DG2 tolerated); limitation of §8 (zeroing of DG1, DG2, DG11 and DG12 guaranteed only for Sceau's own arrays).

## D15. Passive and Active Authentication: points left open by the SPEC

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §6.1 steps 5 and 7; decision D6.
- **Decision**:
  - **DS missing from the SOD**: it is looked up in the store by (issuer, serial number) among the anchors, or by key identifier (SKI) if the `SignerIdentifier` has that form. Not found: lines “SOD signature valid” and “Certificate chain” `NOT_AVAILABLE` with the detail `DsCertificateMissing`, “DS certificate validity” `NOT_AVAILABLE`, hence the “Unknown issuer” verdict;
  - **usual period for estimation**: 10 years for all documents, whatever the DG1 code (passports, and CNIe since 2021, for an adult). An **estimated** issue date that falls within the DS period gives `OK`; outside the period, `NOT_AVAILABLE`, never `FAILED` (D6);
  - **calendar validity of CSCAs and link certificates not checked**: only their signatures count in the chain (`CertificateChains`), since a document remains valid up to ten years after its CSCA expires. The DS period, however, is checked at the issue date. Candidate issuers are looked up by Authority Key Identifier then by name (a candidate with the same name but a different SKI is discarded), through at most 8 links, up to a self-signed CSCA in the store;
  - **AA on an EC key without an announced hash algorithm** (DG14 without `ActiveAuthenticationInfo`, a non-standard case met in practice): ECDSA is tried successively with SHA-1, SHA-224, SHA-256, SHA-384 and SHA-512; the first one that verifies gives `OK`. RSA key: ISO/IEC 9796-2 scheme 1, hash designated by the trailer (implicit: SHA-1).
- **Rationale**: never declare an authentic document fake on the basis of an estimate or of the normal expiry of a CSCA; accept real chips that depart from the standard without weakening verification (the signature is still verified with the DG15 key, itself covered by the SOD).
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none; clarifies §6.1 steps 5 and 7.

## D16. Trust store: index, expired CSCA, self-signature, preloading

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §7.1 and §7.2; decision D1.
- **Decision**:
  - **`trust/index.txt`** lists the files in `core/src/main/resources/trust/` (one name per line): listing a classpath directory is not reliable in an APK. `TrustStoreFingerprintTest` checks that the index, the directory and `docs/trust-store.md` list the same files;
  - the **ANTS 2010 passport CSCA**, expired in March 2026, is kept: it signed DSs whose passports, valid for ten years, are still in circulation (see D15, CSCA validity not checked);
  - **`TrustAnchor.isSelfSigned`** is inferred from the key identifiers: subject equal to issuer and AKI equal to SKI, or AKI absent and SKI present; the signature is only verified as a fallback, when these extensions do not allow a conclusion. Verifying the signature of the 590 anchors (including 160 EC keys with explicit parameters) took about 35 s on a Fairphone 3 when opening the Trust store screen; the result is identical on all 590 anchors, in under a second. Building the DS → CSCA chain still verifies every signature (D15);
  - **preloading**: `SceauApplication` loads the store in the background when the process starts, so that neither the first read nor the Trust store screen has to wait. A preloading error is ignored: the next access retries loading and reports it.
- **Rationale**: reliable loading in an APK; verification of documents that are still valid; performance on an old phone.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none; clarifies §7.1 and §7.2.

## D17. `:testchip` module and demo mode restricted to the debug APK

- **Date**: 2026-09-25.
- **Context**: SPEC §2 plans two modules (`:core`, `:app`) and §9.1 a test factory. This factory, a chip simulator and a specimen document serve both for `:core` tests and for demonstrating the interface without a real document.
- **Decision**:
  - Kotlin JVM module **`:testchip`** (package `io.github.mgdx.sceau.testchip`): dummy PKI (old and new CSCA, link, DS, SOD, in RSA and EC), stateful simulated chip (`SimulatedChip`: EF.CardAccess, BAC, PACE with ECDH generic mapping, 3DES and AES secure messaging, Chip Authentication, Active Authentication) written without JMRTD's protocol code, and specimen CNIe (`SimulatedDocuments.frenchIdCard()`: identity SPECIMEN / MARIANNE, CAN 123456, synthetic JPEG 2000 portrait). `:core` uses it as `testImplementation` only; its main code does not depend on it. See `testchip/README.md`;
  - **demo mode**: “Simulate a French ID card (demo)” entry in the home screen menu, in the debug APK only. `:testchip` is a `debugImplementation` there; the `DemoMode` object exists in two versions: `app/src/debug/` reads the simulated CNIe, `app/src/release/` is empty (`isAvailable = false`, the menu entry is not shown). The demo read follows the same path as a real document's (`SessionViewModel.launchRead`), with the same wiping rules, and the result screen shows the “Simulated document — demo” banner;

The entered key is forgotten when the demo starts, so that a real document presented afterwards is never read with the demo's key or store;

  - lint: `TrulyRandom` disabled in `:testchip` (fixed-seed randomness, intended for reproducible keys); `@Suppress("GetInstance")` on `SimCrypto.aesBlock` (single-block AES encryption required by ICAO 9303-11), reported by the `:app` lint, which analyzes its dependencies.
- **Rationale**: test `:core` against an independent implementation of the chip; allow demonstrations and interface testing without a real identity document, without any test key or certificate reaching the distributed APK.

## D20. Chip Authentication before reading the data

- **Date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Context**: SPEC §6.1 lists the reading of DG1, DG2, DG14, DG15, DG11 and DG12 (step 4), then Passive Authentication (step 5), then Chip Authentication (step 6). Performed after reading, CA did not bind to the authenticated chip the data read before it, under the PACE or BAC keys: a limitation documented by the security audit of 2026-09-25.
- **Decision**:
  - reading order: EF.COM, EF.SOD, then DG14 if announced (EF.COM or SOD), then Chip Authentication if DG14 announces a key, then DG1, DG2, DG15, DG11 and DG12 under the CA's secure messaging, then Passive Authentication (which still verifies the DG14 hash: a forged DG14 makes the hashes line fail), then Active Authentication;
  - CA takes place during the `READ_DATA` step; `Step` is unchanged, `VERIFY_CHIP` is still emitted and now covers only AA. CA error codes start with `READ_DATA-CA` instead of `VERIFY_CHIP-CA`;
  - the explicit confirmation of the new channel (SELECT of DG1 and READ BINARY of one byte, MAC verified) is kept before reading the DGs: it is this confirmation, and not the subsequent reading of DG1, that decides the CA line, so that a clone produces a failed CA line and not a reading error;
  - failed CA: `FAILED` line, and reading continues. If the chip refused the exchange, JMRTD keeps the old secure messaging; the same confirmation checks that the chip still responds to it. If the chip no longer responds under the current secure messaging, the link is reset (`CardTransport.reconnect()`, new JMRTD state), then the channel is re-established with the same key within the same read (EF.CardAccess, PACE or BAC, fallback included: `SecureChannel.reestablish`), and the DGs are read under that channel. The key is not kept beyond the read;
  - rule V1 unchanged: DG14 or DG15 signed in the SOD but not provided give a failure;
  - `readAndVerify`, `VerificationReport`, `CheckId` and `Step` are unchanged. Tests: `ChipAuthenticationOrderTest` (the simulated chip records under which session each file is served).
- **Rationale**: ICAO 9303-11 inspection procedure (§6.2): CA follows the reading of DG14 and precedes that of the other data, which are then read under keys that only the chip holding the DG14 private key can derive. A relay or a chip that would substitute the data after authentication is thus ruled out. Continuing to read after a failed CA makes it possible to display the data, the verdict remaining “Failed”.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1, order of steps 4 to 6 (CA precedes the reading of DG1, DG2, DG15, DG11, DG12 and Passive Authentication); §6.1 step 6 clarified (channel recovery after a failed CA).

## D21. PACE-CAM (Chip Authentication Mapping)

- **Date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Context**: ICAO 9303-11 §4.4 defines the PACE-CAM mapping, through which the chip proves during PACE that it holds its Chip Authentication private key. `SecureChannel` accepted any announced mapping without checking anything specific to CAM: with a CAM chip, the proof provided was ignored. SPEC §6.1 only plans CA via DG14.
- **Decision**:
  - if PACE succeeds with the CAM mapping, EF.CardSecurity (FID 011D at the MF) is read under PACE secure messaging before the application is selected; JMRTD decrypts `CA_IC` (`PACECAMResult`) and provides `PK_Map,IC`;
  - at the `VERIFY_CHIP` step, once the issuing State of DG1 is known: EF.CardSecurity is verified with the same rules as the SOD (`PassiveAuthenticator.verifyCardSecurity`, which reuses the DS lookup, the signature verification and `CertificateChains`: issuer profile, DS keyUsage, consistent countries, weak algorithms rejected), its `ChipAuthenticationPublicKeyInfo` gives `PK_IC`, then `PK_Map,IC = KA(CA_IC, PK_IC, D_IC)` is checked (ECDH or DH, depending on the keys);
  - result on the `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` line (no new `CheckId`), with a new detail `CheckDetail.ChipAuthentication(method)` (`DG14` or `PACE_CAM`) shown in the checklist. Successful CAM: CA via DG14 is not redone, the data having already been read under the PACE channel. CAM performed but EF.CardSecurity missing, malformed, with a wrong signature or chain, or a wrong KA check: `FAILED` (codes `VERIFY_CHIP-CAM-…`). Unknown algorithm: `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM`. Verdict unchanged (`Verdicts.compute`);
  - EF.CardSecurity issuer missing from the store: `NOT_AVAILABLE` if the SOD chain also has no known CSCA (verdict “Unknown issuer”, like any document from a country missing from the store), `FAILED` otherwise;
  - the calendar validity of the EF.CardSecurity DS is not checked (it is for the SOD DS, “DS certificate validity” line);
  - `:testchip` simulates PACE-CAM in ECDH (A_IC, EF.CardSecurity signed by the dummy DS). Tests: `PaceCamEndToEndTest`.
- **Rationale**: ICAO 9303-11 §4.4.3.5 requires the terminal, after PACE-CAM, to authenticate `PK_IC` through EF.CardSecurity and to check `CA_IC`; otherwise a cloned chip announcing CAM would pass without proof. Reusing `PassiveAuthenticator` guarantees that EF.CardSecurity is not verified more weakly than the SOD. Treating an unknown issuer like the SOD chain avoids declaring “Failed” an authentic document from a country missing from the store, without opening a downgrade: if the SOD chains up to a known CSCA, an EF.CardSecurity that cannot be chained is a failure.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 6 (Chip Authentication can also be proven by PACE-CAM, in which case CA via DG14 is not performed); §5.3 (method detail in the “Chip Authentication” line).

## D22. Number and total size of imported Master Lists limited

- **Date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Context**: security audit of 2026-09-25, hardening recommendations (“limit the number of imported Master Lists”). All imported Master Lists are reread and merged when the store is preloaded (D16). Each file is already bounded to 20 MB when read (`MAX_MASTER_LIST_BYTES`, SPEC §5.4), but nothing bounded their number, and hence neither memory nor startup time.
- **Decision**: `ImportLimits` (`app/.../trust/ImportLimits.kt`) sets **at most 10 imported Master Lists** and a **total size of at most 40 MB** (twice the per-file limit, which it reuses). The rule is checked before parsing the file, so as not to ask for confirmation of an import bound to fail, then again under lock at write time (`TrustStoreRepository.import`). Beyond that, the import is refused with a message inviting the user to delete imported certificates. A list already present (same SHA-256 fingerprint, hence same file name) has no effect and does not count, even when the limits are reached. `:core` is not modified.
- **Rationale**: a real Master List weighs on the order of a megabyte (the embedded BSI one: 0.9 MB) and already covers dozens of issuers; ten lists are amply sufficient (a few authorities publishing a Master List, plus their updates), and 40 MB is more than forty times the BSI list. These bounds keep preloading within the memory of an old phone (Fairphone 3) whatever files the user chooses.
- **Update of 2026-10-06** (security audit of 2026-10-06, V22): the limits above bounded size, not parsing cost. A booby-trapped 20 MB Master List could carry any number of SignerInfos (each verified by scanning the whole `certificates` block) and certificates, and this cost was paid at every startup, every read and every opening of the store, deletion being offered only once the store was loaded. From now on:
  - `MasterListParser` (`:core`) requires **exactly one SignerInfo** (`NO_SIGNER` without a signer, `MULTIPLE_SIGNERS` beyond one) and rejects with `TOO_MANY_CERTIFICATES` a list of more than **2,000 certificates** (`TrustStores.MAX_MASTER_LIST_CERTIFICATES`), a cap also applied to the CMS `certificates` block. Both counts apply to the already decoded ASN.1 structure, before any certificate is read. The largest real Master List observed (Sweden, 646 certificates, `docs/trust-sources.md`) and the embedded BSI one (608) remain well under the cap, which is more than three times higher; the embedded list goes through the same check. A list imported before this version that exceeds it is ignored at load time and listed as unreadable, hence deletable.
  - Parsing is **cancellable**: `TrustStores.parseMasterList` calls a checkpoint between costly steps; the import preview (`TrustStoreRepository.preview`) stops when the user leaves the screen.
  - The **list of imported items no longer depends on the merged store**: `TrustStoreRepository` no longer holds its file lock during merging (a second lock only prevents two simultaneous merges), and a store merged during a deletion is not cached. The screen shows the imported items, deletable one by one, during loading and after a failure.
  - The new codes are shown in the existing message “This file isn’t a valid Master List (…)”, without a new string.
  - **2026-10-06**: this generic message (“unreadable format or invalid signature”) was wrong for a readable, properly signed but too large list. `TOO_MANY_CERTIFICATES` now gets a dedicated message that states the cap (`trust_import_master_list_too_many_certificates`, `plurals` in all 45 languages, chosen by `invalidMasterListMessage` in `TrustFormatting.kt`), distinct from the limit of 100 certificates imported on their own (D33). `NO_SIGNER` and `MULTIPLE_SIGNERS` keep the generic message: an ICAO Master List has a single signer, so this really is an invalid format.

## D23. JPEG 2000 decoding in an isolated process

- **Date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Context**: the portrait (DG2) and the DG12 images in JPEG 2000 come from a potentially hostile chip and are decoded by OpenJPEG via JNI (D3). This is the only native surface exposed to hostile bytes; until now it ran in the app process, with access to the data read, to the access key and to the app's files. Hardening recommendation from the security audit of 2026-09-25.
- **Decision**:
  - decoding is delegated to the `jp2/Jpeg2000Service` service, declared in the manifest with `android:isolatedProcess="true"`, `android:exported="false"`, `android:process=":jp2"`: isolated UID, no permission, no access to the app's files or services. `libsceau_jp2.so` is loaded only there: `Jpeg2000Decoder` (unchanged, including the `nativeDecode` method and its R8 rule) is called only by the service. `SceauApplication`, which Android also creates in this process, does nothing there (`isIsolatedProcess()`: `Process.isIsolated()` from API 28, isolated UID range 99000 to 99999 before that);
  - on the app side, `jp2/IsolatedJpeg2000Decoder.decode(context, bytes)` (`suspend`) binds the service, transfers, then unbinds. A fresh process serves a single image: in `onDestroy`, the service wipes its buffers then terminates its process, and with it everything OpenJPEG left in its heap. Decodes remain serialized (a `Mutex` in the client, in addition to the one on the result screen);
  - **transport**: raw binder (`Binder.onTransact`, without AIDL, hence without touching `build.gradle.kts`), interface token checked, in memory only. The stream is sent in 256 KiB chunks (`TX_BEGIN`, `TX_APPEND`), the service replies with width and height (`TX_DECODE`), then the ARGB pixels come back in chunks of 64 Ki pixels (`TX_READ`), each well below the 1 MB limit of the transaction buffer: a 480 × 640 portrait (1.2 MB) goes through in five chunks. `Jpeg2000Protocol` (pure Kotlin, tested on the JVM) sets the bounds: stream from 1 byte to 16 MB, image of at most 2048 × 2048, chunks received in strict order, without overflow. The arrays for the stream, the chunks and the pixels are zeroed on both sides after use;
  - **robustness**: isolated process dead (bomb, native bug: `DeadObjectException`), 10 s timeout exceeded (process startup and transfers included), binding refused or lost, out-of-protocol response: `null`, the image is not displayed, as for an invalid stream. After a timeout, the client unbinds, which terminates the isolated process and unblocks the ongoing transaction; pixels received later are wiped immediately.
- **Rationale**: an OpenJPEG vulnerability exploited by a forged chip now only gives access to a process with no rights, which sees only the current image; the app process can no longer be brought down by a native crash or a memory bomb. Chunking works from API 26, without `SharedMemory` (API 27) or a dual code path. Cost: one process startup per image (a few hundred milliseconds on an old phone) and one more copy of the pixels, negligible for three images at most.
- **Limitations**: the native buffers of binder `Parcel`s (stream and pixels in transit) are freed without zeroing, as the public API does not allow marking a transaction as sensitive; they remain in the memory of both processes, never on disk. A native crash of the isolated process leaves, as before D23 in the app process, a system report (tombstone, readable only by the system, which may contain a few hundred bytes of memory around the registers, hence of the current image) and, depending on the Android version, a crash message: to be checked on a device.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none (SPEC §8 reinforced; complements D3).
- **Update of 2026-10-06 (audit V18)**: the statement in the context was wrong: JPEG 2000 was not the only exposed native surface. Images that were not JPEG 2000 (DG2 portrait, DG7 signature, DG12 images) were decoded by `BitmapFactory` in the app process, and the format declared by the chip took precedence over the bytes: a forged chip declaring `image/jpeg` could have PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, HEIF or any other format recognized by content by the system decoder parsed there (precedent: libwebp, CVE-2023-4863). From now on:
  - the format is never taken from the declared format: `ui/result/detectImageFormat` only recognizes JPEG (`FF D8 FF`) and JPEG 2000 (JP2 signature box, or SOC and SIZ markers of a raw J2K stream); everything else is refused without being decoded, and the screen shows the empty placeholder;
  - JPEG is also decoded in the isolated process, by the same service (`Jpeg2000Service`, name and `:jp2` process kept so as not to touch the manifest) and the same binder protocol. The service redoes the detection on the received bytes, regardless of what the app believes it sent: JPEG to `BitmapFactory`, with the bounds from audit V2 (`inJustDecodeBounds`, `MAX_DECLARED_PIXELS`, `inSampleSize`, reduction to 2048 pixels per side), JP2 or J2K to OpenJPEG, anything else refused. Client-side validation of the response is unchanged. No image byte coming from the chip reaches a native decoder in the app process any more; `libsceau_jp2.so` is still not loaded there;
  - cost: one more isolated process per JPEG image (DG7 signature of the CNIe, portrait of documents that encode it in JPEG), that is, a few hundred milliseconds on an old phone. Residual risk: a vulnerability in the system JPEG decoder exploited in the isolated process, with no rights, which sees only the current image.
- **Update of 2026-10-06 (audit V26)**: the termination of the process after a timeout was described inaccurately. `onDestroy` wiped the buffers under the lock that the binder thread holds throughout a transaction, decoding included: after a timeout, it waited for decoding to finish, and `Process.killProcess` only came afterwards. The isolated process kept the image in memory and consumed CPU and memory beyond the 10 s, with a risk of a service ANR around 20 s. Now `onDestroy` never waits for the lock (`ReentrantLock.tryLock`): it wipes the buffers if they are free, which is the case on a normal termination, otherwise leaves them to disappear with the process, then terminates it in every case. Unbinding the client therefore ends the isolated process even in the middle of decoding, as described under robustness above.

## D24. Reduced R8 rules for BouncyCastle, JMRTD, SCUBA and cert-cvc

- **Date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Context**: `rules.keep` kept in full (`-keep class … { *; }`) the BouncyCastle provider packages (`jcajce.provider`, `jce.provider`), JMRTD, SCUBA and cert-cvc. R8 could neither remove their unused members nor obfuscate them (recommendation from the audit of 2026-09-25).
- **Decision**:
  - BouncyCastle: `-keep class org.bouncycastle.jcajce.provider.** { <init>(...); }` and likewise for `org.bouncycastle.jce.provider.**`. Only class names and their constructors are kept. The provider loads its `$Mappings` classes by name (`ClassUtil.loadClass` then `newInstance`), then registers each implementation as a class name, which `Provider.Service` instantiates through reflection. The methods called afterwards override Java SPIs (`CipherSpi`, `SignatureSpi`, `KeyFactorySpi`…) or `AlgorithmProvider.configure`, which R8 keeps on its own for an instantiated class. `<init>(...)` rather than `<init>()` covers services constructed with a parameter (`CertStore`). Inventory made on BC 1.86: 1,403 registered services, 1,361 of them in these two packages; the other 42 (post-quantum, `org.bouncycastle.pqc.jcajce.provider`) were already not kept and remain absent, since Sceau does not use them. The `SupportedKeyClasses` attributes name only JDK interfaces. BC's other reflective uses (`GcmSpecUtil`, `SpecUtil`, `BaseBlockCipher`, `DRBG`, `RFC3280CertPathUtilities`) target JDK classes.
  - JMRTD, SCUBA and cert-cvc: no rules at all any more. The bytecode of JMRTD 0.8.8, SCUBA 0.0.21 and cert-cvc 1.4.13 makes only three reflective calls. `Country.values()` calls `values()` on SCUBA's enumerations, which is covered by the `enum` rule of `proguard-android-optimize.txt`. `CardService.getInstance` instantiates `IsoDepCardService` or `TerminalCardService` by name, and `CVCProvider` is a JCA provider: Sceau uses neither (`TransportCardService` extends `CardService` directly). The logger names switched off by Sceau (`org.jmrtd`, `net.sf.scuba`) are string literals in these libraries, hence unaffected by obfuscation. The `getSimpleName()` calls in a few JMRTD `toString()` methods are only for display.
  - Unchanged: `-dontwarn javax.naming.**` and the JNI rule for `Jpeg2000Decoder`.
- **Rationale**: unsigned release APKs (`./gradlew :app:assembleRelease`), in bytes:

  | APK | Before | After | Gain |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | arm64-v8a | 3,661,003 | 3,464,396 | −196,607 (−5.4%) |
  | armeabi-v7a | 3,611,019 | 3,414,412 | −196,607 (−5.4%) |
  | x86 | 3,698,123 | 3,501,516 | −196,607 (−5.3%) |
  | x86_64 | 3,696,221 | 3,499,614 | −196,607 (−5.3%) |
  | universal | 4,466,750 | 4,270,143 | −196,607 (−4.4%) |

  `classes.dex` goes from 5,561,300 to 5,058,276 bytes (−9%). According to `mapping.txt`, cert-cvc disappears (62 classes, never reached), JMRTD goes from 235 to 131 classes and SCUBA from 40 to 17, all obfuscated; BouncyCastle classes outside the provider are shrunk, and those of the provider lose their unused members. Most of BC remains: each registered service references its engine. Going further would require replacing `BouncyCastleProvider` with a provider restricted to ICAO algorithms, which touches the `:core` code and is outside the scope of this decision. Keeping only the registered classes through an explicit list would gain little (abstract classes remain anyway as supertypes) and would break at every BC upgrade.

  Verification of the minified code, done by hand (JVM tests do not go through R8): R8 (`build-tools/37.0.0/lib/d8.jar`, `--classfile` mode, JDK 17 as library) minifies `core.jar`, `testchip.jar`, their dependencies and a small check program, with `proguard-android-optimize.txt`, the coroutines rules and `rules.keep`. Only the resources (`trust/`, specimen portrait) are put back alongside, without any non-minified class. The program then runs on the minified code: loading the embedded store (BSI CMS Master List, 590 anchors), importing that same Master List, then `readAndVerify` on the simulated chip in five variants: CNIe PACE CAN and PACE MRZ (brainpoolP256r1, AES secure messaging, ECDSA DS, AES CA, ECDSA AA), then BAC passports (3DES) with RSA PKCS#1, RSA-PSS and ECDSA CSCA/DS, RSA CA and AA. Result: Authentic verdict and all seven checks OK everywhere. Negative control: without the two BC rules, the same program fails as soon as loading starts (`CertificateException: X.509 not found`). Limitations: R8 targeted the JDK and not `android.jar`, and `:testchip` was minified with the rest, so it may keep JMRTD members that Sceau alone would not need. The check therefore does not replace a test of the release APK on a device: startup (preloading), import of a Master List and reading of a real CNIe and a real passport.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none. The SPEC does not set R8 rules; the gain contributes to the target of under 8 MB per APK (SPEC §2, D2).

## D25. Reproducible builds of the release APKs

- **Date**: 2026-09-29.
- **Context**: to publish on F-Droid the APKs signed by the developer (rather than APKs signed by F-Droid), F-Droid must rebuild from source APKs that are identical byte for byte ([Reproducible Builds](https://f-droid.org/docs/Reproducible_Builds/)). Measurement made on commit `ea42fbf`: two clones built in different paths (`./gradlew :app:assembleRelease`, same JDK, NDK and CMake) gave different APKs for all five outputs. Only `lib/<abi>/libsceau_jp2.so` differed, by 20 bytes: the `.note.gnu.build-id` note. The linker computes the build-id on the unstripped `.so`, whose debug information contains the absolute paths of the sources (`app/src/main/cpp`, OpenJPEG included), of the `.cxx` build directory and of the NDK headers; stripping removes this information but keeps the build-id. Everything else (`classes.dex`, `resources.arsc`, `baseline.prof`, `META-INF/version-control-info.textproto`, order and timestamps of ZIP entries) was already identical.
- **Decision**:
  - `app/src/main/cpp/CMakeLists.txt`: `-ffile-prefix-map` (which covers `__FILE__` and debug information) replaces the source root with `src`, the CMake build directory with `build` and the NDK root with `ndk`, through `add_compile_options` placed before `add_subdirectory(openjpeg)` so as to apply to both targets. The build-id is kept (useful to symbolize a native trace); it becomes stable;
  - `app/build.gradle.kts`: `dependenciesInfo { includeInApk = false; includeInBundle = false }`. This block, added by AGP to the signing block of signed APKs and encrypted for Google Play, does not appear in unsigned APKs but would make the developer's signed APK differ from F-Droid's, which rejects it anyway;
  - `scripts/check-reproducible.sh` performs the double build (two clones at the same commit, paths of different lengths, SDK seen under another path for the second) and compares the APKs; procedure and versions to pin in `docs/reproducible-builds.md`.
- **Rationale**: the measurement after the fix, with a clone and an SDK under two different paths, gives five APKs identical byte for byte (SHA-256 of the unsigned APKs, measured on an intermediate commit of this branch, since they change at each commit through `version-control-info.textproto`: arm64-v8a `366a9eba…`, armeabi-v7a `e346e1f6…`, x86 `00430b49…`, x86_64 `13f3bfb7…`, universal `0ccddb62…`). The negative control (same script on the fix without the NDK remapping) shows that changing the SDK path alone is enough to make all five APKs differ: the NDK remapping is necessary, since F-Droid does not install the SDK in the same location as the developer. APK sizes are unchanged to the byte (the build-id has a fixed size). `-Wl,--build-id=none` would also have removed the difference, at the cost of symbolization and without removing the absolute paths from the unstripped `.so`.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none. The SPEC targets F-Droid (§2) and publishes the APKs on the releases (§10) without setting the signing mode; reproducible builds make it possible to publish the APKs signed by the developer. Limitation: reproducibility is verified on Linux x86_64; a build on another host platform (NDK `prebuilt/darwin-x86_64` prefix, different compiler binary) is not guaranteed to be identical.

## D28. Automatic check for text overflow in all languages

- **Date**: 2026-09-29.
- **Context**: the interface is translated into 45 languages (D27), with no possible visual review: `FLAG_SECURE` forbids any screenshot on a device, and reviewing 45 languages by hand at each string change is not sustainable. A label too long for its tab, a truncated title or text running off the screen would go unnoticed.
- **Decision**:
  - JVM test `TextOverflowTest` (`app/src/test/java/io/github/mgdx/sceau/l10n/`), run by `./gradlew check` via `:app:testDebugUnitTest`: Robolectric 4.17 with its native rendering (`GraphicsMode.NATIVE`: Android's real text engine and its fonts, non-Latin scripts included) and Compose UI test; test dependencies only, plus `ui-test-manifest` as `debugImplementation` (see `docs/dependencies.md`);
  - 360 × 640 dp screen at xxhdpi, font at 100%, simulated SDK 37 (the highest Robolectric 4.17 supports, equal to `compileSdk`);
  - languages read from `res/xml/locales_config.xml` at each run; the language and writing direction (Arabic, Hebrew) are changed through the configuration `CompositionLocal`s above the real screens, without recreating the activity. Safeguard: each language must resolve its own strings, and `ar` and `iw` must be written right to left;
  - real screens, with the real `SessionViewModel` and the real trust store: Home (Identity card tab with NFC disabled, overflow menu open, Passport tab without NFC with the three date errors), Reading (chip making you wait, obtained by holding the simulated CNIe while the secure channel is being opened; six errors: `ACCESS_DENIED`, `CAN_WITHOUT_PACE`, `NOT_ICAO`, `CONNECTION_LOST`, `TIMEOUT`, `UNEXPECTED`), Result (simulated CNIe from demo mode, Authentic and Unknown issuer verdicts, checks expanded; portrait unavailable, since the JPEG 2000 decoder runs in an isolated process that is absent under Robolectric), Trust store (full list, France and United Kingdom expanded), About;
  - for each text node (unmerged tree): line wider than the text box or text taller than it, last line ellipsized, word broken across two lines for lack of width (space-separated scripts only), box running out of a container (tab, bar) or off the screen; a scrolling container stops the check along its axis;
  - all anomalies are collected and then compared with `app/src/test/resources/l10n/known-overflows.txt` (`language|key`): failure if an anomaly is not listed there, or if a listed line no longer occurs. A second test checks on dummy texts that each kind of overflow is indeed reported;
  - `:app` tests run on JDK 25 (that of the Gradle daemon and of CI), with `--add-exports java.base/jdk.internal.access` as Robolectric requires on that JDK; the code is still compiled for Java 17.
- **Rationale**:
  - Robolectric rather than an instrumented test: `check` runs without a device or emulator, in CI as well as locally. Measured duration: about 20 s for the 45 languages and 16 screens or states (excluding the initial download of Robolectric's Android jars).
  - `TextLayoutResult.hasVisualOverflow` is not used for width: the result provided by the semantics is laid out again over the full width of the constraints, so that `didOverflowWidth` is true for any text narrower than its container. Width is compared line by line, with a tolerance of 1 dp (centered lines are rounded to the whole pixel by Android); `didOverflowHeight` is kept.
  - The broken-word and container-overflow checks were added after a negative control: three deliberately oversized German labels (tab, titles) were not reported, because Material 3 components grow in height instead of clipping. With these checks, the oversized tab and single-word title are reported (broken word), without any false alarm across the 45 real languages; the multi-word title, wrapped onto three lines in an enlarged bar, is not (see Limitations).
  - Language changed within the composition rather than by recreating the activity: a single trust store load and a single simulated read per state, hence the duration.
- **Limitations**:
  - an app bar title on several lines is not reported: the Material 3 bar grows and nothing is clipped;
  - data excluded: none. Values coming from the chip (`FieldRow`, check details) have an explicit `maxLines` and an intended ellipsis, but those of the specimen are short and do not reach it;
  - not covered: “document expired” banner, “imported anchor” mention, “Valid signature, chip not verified” and “Failed” verdicts, displayed photo, Master List import and deletion dialogs, one-off messages (snackbars);
  - Robolectric's font rendering is that of Android 37; a manufacturer that replaces the system fonts may widen some texts.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none on the product. The SPEC does not plan this check; it adds test dependencies (outside the APK) and an empty test activity in the debug APK only.

## D33. Import and one-by-one deletion of certificates and Master Lists

- **Date**: 2026-10-04.
- **Context**: SPEC §5.4 only allowed importing a signed CMS Master List, and clearing everything at once. Yet several States publish their CSCA and their link certificates one by one on their website (this is how the embedded certificates of D26 were obtained), without a Master List. The user could neither add them nor remove a single imported Master List.
- **Decision**:
  - **New trust source** `TrustSource.IMPORTED_CERTIFICATE`, distinct from `IMPORTED_MASTER_LIST`: a standalone certificate is vouched for by no State signature; it is the user who grants it trust. Merged at the lowest priority (ANTS, national publications, embedded Master List, imported Master Lists, imported certificates): a certificate already present keeps its original source. Label “Imported certificate” in the store list and in the chain detail of the result.
  - **Checks** (`TrustStores.parseCertificate`, `:core`, `InvalidCertificateException` with stable codes): exactly one X.509 certificate, in DER with no extra byte or in PEM with a single `CERTIFICATE` block and no other block, with a readable public key (`UNREADABLE`); authority certificate, `basicConstraints` with cA=TRUE and `keyUsage` with keyCertSign if present (`NOT_CA`, since ICAO 9303-12 requires these extensions for CSCAs and links); self-signature verified for a certificate presenting itself as self-signed, that is, subject equal to issuer and AKI absent or equal to SKI (`BAD_SIGNATURE`). The signature of a link, made by the old key, is still verified when building the chain, which only ever anchors a chain on a self-signed CSCA: a link imported on its own only extends trust from an already known CSCA. Calendar validity is not required, as for the other anchors (D15).
  - **Preview before import**: type (self-signed CSCA or link), subject, country, validity and SHA-256 fingerprint, with a warning: no State signature vouches for this file, an imported certificate can make any document from its country authentic, compare the fingerprint with the one published by the State.
  - **Storage**: `filesDir/trust/<SHA-256 of the DER>.der`, in DER (a PEM is converted before writing), next to the `.ml` files; certificate rechecked just before the atomic write; a file that has become invalid is ignored at load time. The file name equals the displayed fingerprint, and the same certificate imported in DER then in PEM does not create a duplicate.
  - **Limits** (`ImportLimits`, as in D22): at most 100 imported certificates, 64 KiB per file, counted separately from Master Lists. A CSCA weighs on the order of a kilobyte; a hundred certificates amply cover the States missing from Master Lists without weighing on preloading (D16).
  - **Interface: a single button** “Import a Master List or a certificate”, which recognizes the file. A file of at most 64 KiB is first read as a certificate (reading a single ASN.1 object, without costly computation); if it is not one, it follows the Master List path (D22 limits, then CMS verification). A file that is neither receives its own message. One button rather than two: the user does not have to know how to tell a `.der` containing a certificate from a `.der` containing a Master List (both extensions are in circulation), the system picker is the same, and the two confirmation dialogs remain distinct.
  - **Duplicates refused at preview** (2026-10-04, anomalies found on a Fairphone 3: an embedded ANTS certificate could be imported, and a certificate re-imported in PEM after DER reopened the preview, then “Import” silently did nothing). Before any confirmation dialog and without writing anything, `TrustStoreRepository` refuses with `DuplicateImportException` (pure logic in `trust/ImportDuplicates`):
    - a certificate whose DER SHA-256 fingerprint is that of a certificate imported on its own (file `<empreinte>.der`, hence regardless of whether the chosen file is DER or PEM): “This certificate has already been imported.”;
    - a certificate already present in the loaded store (`TrustStore.anchors`, highest-priority source): “This certificate is already in the trust store (source: ANTS / national publication / built-in Master List / imported Master List). There is nothing to import.” One complete sentence per source, for translation. A certificate present only in an imported Master List is refused as well: it would bring nothing as long as the list remains imported;
    - a Master List whose file SHA-256 fingerprint is that of an imported Master List (name of the `.ml` file): “This Master List has already been imported.”, or that of the embedded Master List: dedicated message. The fingerprint of the embedded list is computed once by the app on the `:core` resource, enumerated through `trust/index.txt` as the loader does: no API added to `:core`. The duplicate is detected before the D22 limits: re-importing a list already present when ten are imported gives “already imported”, not “limit reached”.
    The same checks are redone under lock at write time (`import`, `importCertificate`). A different Master List whose certificates are all already known remains importable (it may be more recent); the preview states how many of its certificates are missing from the current store (“12 new certificates out of 590”, distinct certificates counted by DER fingerprint).
  - **Imported items**: new section of the screen, one row per Master List (signer, signing date, number of certificates) or certificate (subject, country, validity, fingerprint), with one-by-one deletion after confirmation. The identifier is the file name; `TrustStoreRepository.removeImported` refuses any identifier that is not `<64 lowercase hex digits>.ml` or `.der` (path traversal). A file that has become unreadable remains listed so that it can be deleted. “Delete imported certificates” still clears everything.
- **Rationale**: add a CSCA published by a State without waiting for a release or for a Master List containing it, without weakening the rest of the store: distinct source with the lowest priority, profile and self-signature checks, explicit warning, displayed fingerprint, bounded limits.
  - **Update of 2026-10-06** (security audit of 2026-10-06, V23): the X.500 subjects read from an imported file (signer of a Master List, subject of a certificate) were displayed as rendered by `X500Principal.getName()`, which escapes neither line breaks nor bidirectional control characters: a subject could imitate a fake “Signer’s SHA-256 fingerprint” line in the confirmation dialog. In both import dialogs, the list of imported items and the store list, these subjects now go through `displaySafe` (fix V12 of the Result screen): a single logical line without control or formatting characters, at most 256 code points and 4 displayed lines, with ellipsis. An unrecognized country code is sanitized in the same way (8 code points). Fingerprints, computed by the app, are not truncated.
  - **Verdict card**: `CheckFormatting.isImportedAnchor` recognizes both imported sources; the mention “Verified using a certificate you imported” (audit V6) is therefore also shown for a chain that ends at a certificate imported on its own. The label suits both cases: an imported Master List also brings imported certificates.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.4 supplemented.

## D34. Known issuer deviations: hardcoded rule, Italian case

- **Date**: 2026-10-04 (user's decisions: hardcoded rule, then broad criterion).
- **Context**: Italy publishes a Deviation List (ICAO 9303-12, `id-icao-DeviationList`, `docs/trust-sources.md` §2.4): 346,275 CIE 3.0 identity cards carry a DG12 whose hash does not match the SOD, and whose issue date is 2015-12-05. Sceau gave “Failed” on these authentic cards.
- **Decision**:
  - **hardcoded rule, no embedded file**: the TDDL (SHA-256 `1219d8c74a7acf13c55e265fa8125230167160f4f999b64b28644275a084d31d`, signed on 2018-05-30) was downloaded, its CMS signature verified up to the Italian CSCA03, then decoded; it is not embedded. The Italian source is classified B for reuse (`docs/trust-sources.md`), and its 346,275 document numbers, which do not form ranges, would weigh from 125 KB to 3 MB: they are not included;
  - **generic registry** `KnownDeviations` (`core/…/verify/KnownDeviations.kt`), internal, injected into `PassiveAuthenticator` (real registry by default, another registry in tests). Each rule carries a stable identifier without personal data (`KnownDeviation`, here `IT-CIE3-DG12`), its source, and the date and SHA-256 fingerprint of the published list;
  - **Italian criterion**, broader than the list, all cumulative: CSCA from country IT; issuing State `ITA` and document code starting with `C` (type `C` in the list: first character of the card's MRZ code) in DG1; number of the form `CA` + 5 digits + 2 letters, the form of all listed numbers; SOD signingTime present and between 2017-09-01 and 2018-03-05. This one-month margin around the list's issuance period (2017-10-01 to 2018-02-05) covers the gap between the SOD signature, at personalization, and the issue date recorded by the issuer. Optional restriction on unverified data: a readable DG12 whose issue date is not 2015-12-05 is not covered. This data only serves to narrow the rule, never to broaden it;
  - **safeguards**:
    - a rule can only target DG11 or DG12: the constructor refuses it for any other DG, and the application filters again. A deviation on DG1, DG2, DG14, DG15 or EF.CardSecurity is never tolerated;
    - a deviation is only looked for if the SOD signature and the chain are valid (`OK`), the DG1 hash verified and no signed DG missing. The criterion therefore only relies on signed elements;
    - a deviation is only reported if the targeted DG actually deviates;
  - **effect**: the tolerated DG leaves `DataGroupHashes.mismatched` and appears in `DataGroupHashes.deviations`; the “Data group hashes” line reports it (“known issue published by the issuer, data discarded”). The DG is discarded (`PassiveAuthResult.discardedDataGroups`): `ReadingSession` removes it from `DocumentData` and zeroes its bytes and images. The DG12 issue date is not used for DS validity, which falls back on signingTime. The verdict is computed normally: “Authentic” if the rest is good.
  - **expiry** (2026-10-04, user's request): each rule carries an `obsoleteAfter` date after which no covered document can still be valid. The rule only applies to a document whose DG1 expiry date (signed) does not exceed it. An unreadable expiry also excludes the document. For `IT-CIE3-DG12`, the date chosen is **2029-03-05**. Calculation: latest issuance accepted by the criterion (2018-03-05, signingTime with the margin), plus 10 years, the maximum validity of an identity card for an adult (decree-law 112/2008, art. 31, converted by law 133/2008), plus at most one year, since the expiry is postponed to the holder's next birthday (decree-law 5/2012, art. 7, para. 2, converted by law 35/2012). The cards in the list, issued up to 2018-02-05, expire on 2029-02-05 at the latest. The 50-year validity beyond age 70 only applies to cards issued since 2026-07-30. A test in `KnownDeviationsTest` fails, **and therefore CI does too**, as soon as the current date (UTC) passes `obsoleteAfter`: the rule and this decision must then be removed;
- **Rationale**: the scope is broader than the list, without risk for the verdict. Only DG12, supplementary data with no role in verification, has its deviation tolerated, and it is then neither displayed nor used. The elements on which the verdict is based (SOD, chain, DG1, DG2, DG14, DG15) remain verified without exception. Hardcoding the rule avoids embedding a file whose reuse status is uncertain.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 5 (a wrong DG12 hash always gave “Failed”) clarified by this bounded exception.

## D35. First-launch introduction and “What the chip contains” screen

- **Date**: 2026-10-04 (user's request; independent review incorporated the same day).
- **Context**: the user of an application that reads their identity document wants to know what it reads, what it checks and what it keeps. Nothing explained this before the result, and the About screen mostly talks about the license and the trust store.
- **Decision**:
  - new package `ui/learn`: 4-page swipeable introduction (`HorizontalPager`): the ICAO 9303 chip and its signed copy of the data; the access key (CAN on the front of most recent cards, or MRZ on the passport photo page or the back of the card) and the PACE or BAC encrypted channel; what Sceau checks (Passive Authentication with the embedded or imported certificates, Chip or Active Authentication) and the four verdicts, with the labels and explanations of the result screen (`result_verdict_*`) so that they remain identical; privacy (no Internet permission, nothing saved, wiping, screenshots blocked);

## D36. German cards: chip reserved for authorities (DE-1) and eID-UB card without identity (DE-2)

- **Date**: 2026-10-04 (batch V2-B, cases DE-1 and DE-2 of `docs/deviations.md` §3.1, source BSI TR-03127 v1.40).
- **Context**:
  - DE-1: a German identity card issued before 2021-08-02 accepts PACE with the CAN, but reserves the ICAO application and EF.SOD for government terminals (Terminal Authentication, TA2). The chip answers `6982` to the selection of the application under secure messaging or to the reading of EF.SOD (inferred from TR-03127, not verified on a real card). The user saw “An unexpected error occurred” (`UNEXPECTED-…-6982`) and a useless “Try again”.
  - DE-2: the eID card for EU citizens (eID-UB) carries in DG1 a replacement MRZ (code “UB”, State “D”, “<” everywhere else) and in DG2 the eID logo, identical for all cards. The signature can be valid.
- **Decision**:
  - **DE-1, new code `ACCESS_RESTRICTED`** (`SceauException.AccessRestricted`), only **after a secure channel has been established**: SW `6982` on selection of the ICAO application under secure messaging (`SecureChannel.selectApplet`, element `SELECT_APPLET`), or on reading an essential file, EF.SOD or DG1 (`DocumentReader.readRequired`, elements `SOD` and `DG1`). The diagnostic follows D11: `ReadingSession` prepends the step (`ACCESS_RESTRICTED-SECURE_CHANNEL-SELECT_APPLET`, `ACCESS_RESTRICTED-READ_DATA-SOD`, `ACCESS_RESTRICTED-READ_DATA-DG1`). No JMRTD exception is attached;
  - **unchanged**: a `6982` before authentication (selection of the application in the clear, before BAC) remains `UNEXPECTED-CONNECT-SELECT_APPLET-CardServiceException-6982`; a key rejection during PACE or BAC remains `ACCESS_DENIED`; a `6982` on EF.COM, DG2, DG11, DG12, DG14 or DG15 makes the file absent, as before (optional read; if it is signed in the SOD, hashes, CA or AA fail, audit V1);
  - **deviation from the batch**: EF.COM is **not** treated as essential. It has been optional in `DocumentReader` since the Socle milestone (unsigned, discouraged by the BSI); making it mandatory would make a chip that protects it but serves EF.SOD fail. On a DE-1 card, EF.SOD is refused right afterwards anyway: the code becomes `ACCESS_RESTRICTED-READ_DATA-SOD`;
  - **reading screen**: dedicated message (“This document reserves its chip for authorized officials: Sceau can’t read it, and trying again won’t change that. This is the case for German identity cards issued before August 2021.”), full code shown as for any error, and **no “Try again” button** (`ReadingErrors.canRetry`): only “Cancel” remains, as the primary button;
  - **DE-2, core**: no code change needed, verified by test. `DataGroupParsers.parseDg1` reads the MRZ “UBD<<<…” without exception (empty name, given names and number, null dates, unspecified sex); without DG12, without `signingTime` and without an expiry date, the “DS certificate validity” line is `NOT_AVAILABLE` (never `FAILED`, D15); PA and CA proceed normally, verdict “Authentic”;
  - **DE-2, Result screen** (`IdentityPresentation.isIdentitylessEidUb`): if the SOD signature is valid, the DG1 hash matches, the document code is “UB” and the State “D”, the Identity section is replaced by a banner (“This card’s chip holds no identity data: only its authenticity can be checked. This is the case for the German eID card for EU citizens.”) followed by the only two real pieces of data: the kind of document (“Other travel document (UB)”) and the issuing State (Germany). The SOD signature is required in addition to the hash: a matching hash in an unauthenticated SOD proves nothing. An unauthenticated “UB” DG1 is still displayed like the others (missing values as “Unknown”);
  - **photo hidden**: DG2 is not displayed for an eID-UB. Showing it in the photo frame, under the description “Holder’s photo”, would pass the logo off as a portrait; showing it as a “card image” would teach nothing, since it is the same for all cards. Its hash is still checked and counted in the “Data group hashes” line;
  - **simulated chip**: `SimulatedChip` gets `restrictedApplet` (6982 on selection of the application) and `restrictedFiles` (6982 on selection of these files, even under secure messaging); `SimulatedDocuments.germanEidUb()` builds an eID-UB read through PACE-CAN, with CA and without AA. Its DG1 is written byte by byte, because JMRTD's MRZ constructors compute check digits. Tests: `GermanCardsEndToEndTest` (`:core`), `ReadingErrorsTest` and two `TextOverflowTest` scenes (`ACCESS_RESTRICTED` error, eID-UB result) in all 45 languages (`:app`).
- **Rationale**: the message tells the truth (free software cannot obtain the required terminal certificate) and avoids repeated attempts; the criterion, `6982` after an established channel on an essential element, diverts no other error case. For the eID-UB: display nothing empty or misleading, and say why.
- **Limitation**: neither behavior has been observed on a real card (TP-35, TP-36). The exact SW of a DE-1 card (`6982` assumed) and the actual content of the eID-UB MRZ (TD1 format, “<” in the check digits) remain to be confirmed. Translations made without review by native speakers (as in D27).
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.2 clarified (no “Try again” for `ACCESS_RESTRICTED`); §5.3 clarified (eID-UB: banner in place of the identity, photo hidden); §6.2 supplemented with the `ACCESS_RESTRICTED` code.
- **Update of 2026-10-06 (audit V21)**: the eID-UB presentation additionally requires the SOD certificate chain to be valid (`CERTIFICATE_CHAIN` line OK, hence an “Authentic” or “Valid signature, chip not verified” verdict). A signature verified with a DS that the chip itself provides proves nothing: a forged “UB”/“D” chip signing its SOD with a DS of its own making got the reassuring banner and the hidden photo, under an “Unknown issuer” or “Failed” verdict. It is now presented like any other document (identity and photo displayed). Tests: `IdentityPresentationTest` (`:app`).
- **Update of 2026-10-06 (audit V24, message)**: the `ACCESS_RESTRICTED` code proves nothing about the document, since a forged chip can answer `6982` after PACE to get this message rather than a failure. The message now states the only observed fact (the chip refuses access to its data), cites German cards from before August 2021 as an example and not as an identification of the document, and specifies that this refusal says nothing about its authenticity: “The chip refuses access to its data: Sceau can’t read it, and trying again won’t change that. German identity cards issued before August 2021, for example, reserve their chip for authorized officials. This refusal says nothing about the document’s authenticity.” Reworded in all 45 languages, without review by native speakers (D27). ## D37. Holder's handwritten signature (DG7) read and displayed

- **Date**: 2026-10-04 (user's decision, DG7 batch).
- **Context**: SPEC §6.1 limits reading to DG1, DG2, DG11, DG12, DG14 and DG15. DG7 (ICAO 9303-10 §4.7.7) contains the image of the holder's handwritten signature or usual mark, recorded by some States; the officer checking a document compares it with the one printed on it. The other unread DGs (DG5 other portrait, DG6 reserved, DG8 to DG10 document security features, DG13 country-specific data, DG16 persons to notify) bring nothing verifiable on screen or concern data that Sceau has no business displaying.
- **Decision**:
  - **reading**: DG7 joins `DocumentReader.OPTIONAL_DATA_GROUPS` (order 15, 11, 12, 7): it is requested **only if it is announced** in EF.COM or in the SOD, after Chip Authentication and under its keys (D20). Absent, refused (`6982`) or unreadable: treated as absent, like DG11 and DG12; never an `ACCESS_RESTRICTED` (D36);
  - **decoding** (`DataGroupParsers.parseDg7`): BER lengths checked before JMRTD (`BerStructure.lengthsFit`), strict stream (`StrictInputStream`), `org.jmrtd.lds.icao.DG7File`, `orNull` safety net: an unreadable DG7 is absent, without exception. **First image only**: ICAO allows several images, but documents carry only one, and DG2 likewise only displays its first portrait; a second image would have no distinct label on screen. The format is recognized from the first bytes (`sniffFormat`: JPEG or JPEG 2000), since JMRTD always announces `image/jpeg` for DG7;
  - **model**: `DocumentData.signature: EncodedImage?` (parameter added last, `null` by default), zeroed by `wipe()`; the raw bytes of DG7 are in `rawDataGroups`, hence also zeroed, and kept by the D34 discarding, which only affects DG11 and DG12;
  - **size cap**: 256 KB, like DG2 and DG12 (`FileSizeLimits`, explicit branch): a JPEG signature weighs a few KB, but ICAO bounds neither its size nor the number of images;
  - **check of signed DGs not provided (audit V1)**: DG7 joins `PassiveAuthenticator.READ_DATA_GROUPS` (1, 2, 7, 11, 12, 14, 15). A SOD that announces DG7 without the chip providing it makes `DG_HASHES` fail (`missing = [7]`), and hence the verdict; so does a modified DG7 (`mismatched`). KDoc of `CheckDetail.DataGroupHashes` updated;
  - **D34 safeguard unchanged**: the known deviations registry still only tolerates a hash deviation on DG11 and DG12 (`KnownDeviations.TOLERABLE_DATA_GROUPS`); a deviation on DG7 remains a failure;
  - **Result screen**: “Holder’s signature” card under the identity, only if DG7 is present and readable on the `:core` side. Image decoded through the same path as the photo (`rememberDecodedImage`: JPEG by Android, JPEG 2000 by the isolated service `Jpeg2000Service`, D23), serialized with the other decodes, bitmap handed to the session and wiped like the others (`BitmapOwner`, audit V13). White background in both themes (`ImageBox` takes a background and content color): a signature is generally black on white and would remain unreadable on the dark background. `FLAG_SECURE` is already set on the whole activity. No full screen (the width of the card is enough for a signature);
  - **“What the chip contains” screen** (D35): DG7 moves under “Read and displayed” (“If the country records it: an image of the holder’s handwritten signature.”) and the sentence about unread DGs becomes “DG5, DG6, DG8 to DG10, DG13 and DG16: other portrait, reserved slot, document security features, country-specific data, persons to notify”;
  - **strings**: `result_section_signature`, `learn_chip_dg7` (and `learn_chip_label_dg7`, not translatable) added, `learn_chip_others` revised, in all 45 languages;
  - **simulated chip**: synthetic signature `testchip/src/main/resources/specimen-signature.jpg` (300 × 100, grayscale, 3.5 KB, Bézier curves drawn by `testchip/tools/GenerateSpecimenSignature.java`, no real signature); `TestDataGroups.dg7()` puts it in a DG7 built with JMRTD; the simulated CNIe of demo mode carries this DG7. Tests: `SignatureDg7Test` (provided, withheld, modified, not announced hence never selected, unreadable, oversized, `wipe()`, cap), fuzzing target `dg7` (`LdsParsersFuzzTest`), `DemoModeTest`, and the result scene of `TextOverflowTest` in all 45 languages.
- **Rationale**: display what the chip already signs, useful for visual comparison with the document, without widening the surface more than necessary: DG7 only, only if announced, same defenses as the other image parsers, same wiping chain, and rule V1 extended so that a clone cannot withhold the signed signature.
- **Limitation**: no real document carrying DG7 has been read yet (TP-37); the French CNIe does not carry one. Translations made without review by native speakers (as in D27).
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 4 (list of DGs read extended to DG7); §5.3 (“Holder’s signature” section); §8 (DG7 among the zeroed arrays); §9.1 (DG7 among the signed DGs not provided). SPEC updated with a reference to D37.

- **Date**: 2026-10-06 (author's decisions).
- **Context**: the Publication milestone (SPEC §11) calls for a first release and submission to F-Droid. Still to be set were the version number (`versionName = "1.0"` since the Socle milestone, without a release), the exact license (the README, the SPEC and `docs/dependencies.md` said “GPLv3”, the About screen and the C headers `GPL-3.0-or-later`), hosting, the APKs published on each channel, and the tag format (SPEC §10 said `vX.Y.Z`).

## D40. Hardened verdict path (security audit of 2026-10-06: V17, V19, V20, V24, V25)

- **Date**: 2026-10-06.
- **Context**: the security audit of 2026-10-06 identifies several cases where a cloned or forged chip gets a more favorable verdict than “Failed”, or a “not available” that should be a failure. The fixes do not change the verdict table (`Verdicts.compute`, `docs/protocol.md` §6): they change the status of certain lines.
- **Decision**:
  - **V19, unreadable CA key**: DG14 is reread from its raw bytes (`SecurityInfoProtocols`, without JMRTD), because JMRTD's `SecurityInfo.getInstance` silently discards a `ChipAuthenticationPublicKeyInfo` that it cannot rebuild, or keeps it without a public key (unknown curve). A DG14 that announces a key (OID `id-PK-*`, prefix `0.4.0.127.0.7.2.2.1.`) without a usable key gives `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM` (detail: the OID), hence “Failed”, without sending anything to the chip. Only a DG14 without any `id-PK` leaves the line at `NOT_AVAILABLE`;
  - **V17, EF.CardSecurity and SOD under the same CSCA** (complements D21): with PACE-CAM, the EF.CardSecurity chain must end at the same CSCA as the SOD chain (same SHA-256 fingerprint, or same public key for a CSCA present under two certificates), otherwise `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-CAM-CARD_SECURITY_ANCHOR`). Assumption: both objects are signed by the same State for the same document, under the same CSCA generation; it holds for the specimen CNIe of `:testchip` and excludes no known real document (the DS may differ, not the CSCA). The EF.CardSecurity chain is kept in the report (`VerificationReport.cardSecurityChain`) and the verdict card shows “Verified using a certificate you imported” as soon as either of the two chains relies on an imported anchor (`CheckFormatting.isImportedAnchor`);
  - **V20, downgrade of PACE-CAM or PACE**: EF.CardAccess, which decides the protocol, is not signed. After Passive Authentication, if the DG14 hash is verified, its `PACEInfo`s (OIDs read from the raw bytes) are compared with the channel performed (`ProtocolDowngrade`): DG14 announces PACE-CAM and the channel was established without CAM → `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-DOWNGRADE-CAM`); DG14 announces PACE and the channel is BAC → `SECURE_CHANNEL` `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-DOWNGRADE-BAC`). These rules only apply if neither CA via DG14 nor AA succeeded: a chip that has proven it holds its key is the original whatever protocol was performed, and the PACE → BAC fallback of D13 thus remains without effect on an authentic document that responds to CA or AA (a deliberate departure from the letter of the audit prompt, which did not set this condition). So as not to downgrade by itself a chip that announces several mappings, `SecureChannel` now tries PACE-CAM `PACEInfo`s first. Accepted risk: a document whose DG14 announces PACE, without CA or AA, read by BAC after a transient PACE failure, gets “Failed” instead of “Valid signature, chip not verified”; reading the document again is enough;
  - **V25, known country without a chain** (author's arbitration: “Failed” option): if the store contains at least one certificate (CSCA or link) from the DG1 issuing country and no chain completes (SOD without a DS that can be found in the store, DS under an unknown CSCA), `CERTIFICATE_CHAIN` is `FAILED` with the detail `CheckDetail.NoChainForKnownCountry(country, attempted chain)`, hence the “Failed” verdict; the screen presents it with existing strings (“No CSCA in the trust store matches.”, country, DS attempted or missing), without a new string to translate. “Unknown issuer” (`NOT_AVAILABLE`, D6) remains reserved for countries for which the store contains no certificate, for organization codes (`UNO`, `EUE`…) and for codes unknown to ICAO. In both cases, a SOD signature that the DS verifies but that no chain vouches for is no longer presented as `OK`: `SOD_SIGNATURE` is `NOT_AVAILABLE` (algorithm detail kept). **Accepted risk**: an authentic document signed under a new CSCA generation that the store does not yet contain (renewed CSCA, not yet in the embedded Master List nor published by ANTS) gets “Failed” instead of “Unknown issuer”, without the invitation to import a more recent Master List; updating the store at each release (`scripts/update-trust-store.sh`) and the user importing a Master List or a certificate fix it. This risk is preferred to that of presenting a forgery from a known country as a mere gap in the store, and of pushing the user towards importing a booby-trapped file (V6, V17);
  - **V24, `:core` part: re-establishing the channel after a failed CA** (complements D20 and D36): CA has already failed; any error during re-establishment (reconnection impossible, key refused, timeout, document removed, and `6982`, which gave the `ACCESS_RESTRICTED` error and its message on German cards) produces a report with `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` `FAILED`, hence “Failed”, instead of a reading error. No identity data has been read: the report carries an empty DG1 (only DG14, already read, is in the raw bytes, zeroed with the report); the lines not performed are `NOT_AVAILABLE` with the code `READ_DATA-REESTABLISH-<code de l'erreur>`, without personal data. The rewording of the `ACCESS_RESTRICTED` message (`:app` part of V24) belongs to another fix;
  - **2026-10-06, report without data read** (complements V24): the report carries the explicit flag `VerificationReport.identityRead` (true by default), set to false by `ReadingSession` on this path only, rather than inferring the absence of reading from an empty DG1. The Result screen (`IdentityPresentation.mode`, `Mode.NOT_READ`) then shows neither photo nor photo placeholder, nor Identity section, nor signature, nor supplementary data, but the sentence `result_data_not_read` (“The document data could not be read after the chip verification failed.”, 45 languages), with the verdict, the checklist and the Clear button;
- **Rationale**: a verdict must never become more favorable because a signed element was not understood or because an announced protocol was not performed.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 6 (CA: a DG14 announcing an unusable key is an unsupported algorithm, not an absent step); §5.3 and §6.1 step 5 (“Unknown issuer” limited to countries for which the store contains no certificate; SOD signature not verifiable without a chain); §6.1 step 2 (protocol performed compared with DG14, PACE-CAM tried first).

## D41. Reading bounded in exchanges and duration, NFC responses checked before JMRTD (security audit of 2026-10-07: V29, V36)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: the security audit of 2026-10-07 finds that a forged chip can make reading loop forever by answering `90 00` without data to every `READ BINARY` (V29): SCUBA (`CardFileInputStream`) resends the command as long as the response is empty, JMRTD (`DefaultFileSystem`) accepts an empty response, and `FileSizeLimits` (V11) only bounds the size announced by the header, not the number of exchanges; no global timeout bounded a step outside authentication. It also finds that a forged response in which a secure messaging object announces 2 GB makes JMRTD allocate that array (V36).
- **Decision**:
  - **V29, number of exchanges per file**: `BoundedReadBinarySender` counts, per file (reset at each `SELECT`), consecutive empty responses (at most 3) and `READ BINARY` commands (at most `plafond du fichier / 223 + 16`: 20 for DG1 and EF.COM, 162 for EF.SOD, 89 for EF.CardAccess, DG11, DG14 and DG15, 1,191 for DG2, DG7 and DG12). Beyond that, no other command is sent and reading stops with the new `SceauException.ReadStalled`, with code `READ_STALLED-<STEP>-<file>` (e.g. `READ_STALLED-READ_DATA-DG2`). The exchange cap is derived from the file's size cap, not from the announced size, to leave a wide margin for a real chip that would answer in blocks shorter than 223 bytes. An optional file that no longer progresses interrupts reading instead of being treated as absent: a chip that stops responding is not a chip that lacks the file;
  - **V29, global timeout**: outside authentication, a read has 120 s in total (`ReadingSession.GLOBAL_TIMEOUT_MILLIS`, `ReadDeadline` timer), suspended during the `SECURE_CHANNEL` step, in which PACE and BAC keep their own 60 s per-response timeout (D12); re-establishing the channel after a failed CA (D20, V24), at the `READ_DATA` step, is counted. Since I/O is blocking, the deadline closes the transport, which unblocks the current APDU; reading then ends with `SceauException.Timeout` with code `TIMEOUT-<STEP>-GLOBAL`, even if a report was produced in the meantime (checks done on a closed transport: it is wiped). A complete real read takes from a few seconds to about twenty seconds;
  - **V36, malformed responses**: JMRTD allocates the announced length of a DO'85 or DO'87 (`SecureMessagingWrapper.readDO87`) before verifying the MAC, as well as that of the objects in the response to PACE's GENERAL AUTHENTICATE: a response `87 84 7F FF FF FF 01 …` caused an `OutOfMemoryError`, which went through the core's `catch (Exception)` blocks (app code `UNEXPECTED-OutOfMemoryError`). `TransportCardService.transmit` now checks each response (`ResponseShape`) and throws a `CardServiceException` (converted through the existing path, without a new error class): raw size capped at 64 KiB of data, plus SW (the audit's “raw size of NFC responses” recommendation: otherwise JMRTD's `FragmentBuffer` doubles its buffers); for a protected command (bits `0C` of the CLA, set by JMRTD on every wrapped command), each DO'85 or DO'87 must fit within the data; for GENERAL AUTHENTICATE in the clear, BER lengths must fit (`BerStructure.lengthsFit`). **Departure from the audit prompt**, which proposed `BerStructure.lengthsFit` for secured responses: JMRTD does not read these responses as a sequence of TLVs but byte by byte (an unknown byte is skipped on its own, parsing stops at DO'8E), and a DO'87 hidden in the value of another TLV (`02 05 87 84 7F FF FF | FF 01 …`) passed the BER check and was then read by JMRTD; the check therefore reproduces JMRTD's parsing. The criterion is the command's CLA, not the first byte of the response: the cleartext responses to GET CHALLENGE or to BAC's EXTERNAL AUTHENTICATE are random and can start with `87`. A fuzzing target (`SecureMessagingFuzzTest`, DESede and AES unwrapping) finds the `OutOfMemoryError` without the check;
  - **Reading screen**: `READ_STALLED` reuses the timeout message (“the chip didn’t respond in time”), with “Try again”, without a new string to translate.
- **Rationale**: the availability of reading must not depend on the chip's goodwill, and no response must cause more than its own size to be allocated; the user must see an error and be able to try again, rather than a frozen screen until the document is removed.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.2 and §6.1 (new reading error `READ_STALLED`, global reading timeout).

## D42. Bounded and hardened verification (security audit of 2026-10-07: V30, V31, V35, V40)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: the security audit of 2026-10-07 identifies four weaknesses in offline verification: a computation lasting several minutes, not interruptible, imposed by a giant key (V30); a chain search budget that a hostile import exhausts (V31); an “Unknown issuer” obtained by choosing the DG1 State code (V35); a SOD without signed attributes accepted (V40). None changes the verdict table (`docs/protocol.md` §6).
- **Decision**:
  - **V30, key size**: `Crypto.weakKey` becomes `Crypto.unsupportedKey`, with the existing lower bound (RSA under 1,024 bits, audit V9) and upper bounds: RSA modulus over 8,192 bits or public exponent over 64 bits, DH prime over 8,192 bits, EC base field over 1,024 bits (named curves or explicit parameters, size read from `fieldID` without building the curve). The check comes before any computation for the DS key and that of each issuer in the chain (in their encoded `SubjectPublicKeyInfo` form, before the provider rebuilds and validates the key), the DG14 CA key (before key agreement, nothing is sent to the chip), the EF.CardSecurity keys (encoded form, before JMRTD) and the PACE-CAM mapping key, and the DG15 key for AA. Result: `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM`, detail “algorithm-size” (`RSA-16384`, `RSA-2048/e-65`, `DH-9000`, `EC-2048`), never the key. The bounds are at least twice the largest real keys (RSA 4,096, P-521).
  - **V31, chain search order** (complements audit V15): candidate issuers are sorted by source, in `TrustSource` order (ANTS, national publications, embedded Master List, imported Master List, imported certificate; a certificate present under two sources keeps the safer one), then examined source by source; a self-signed CSCA that verifies the certificate ends the search immediately, before the following candidates are examined. The `MAX_SIGNATURE_CHECKS` budget (64) is unchanged: it can no longer be exhausted by imported decoys when an embedded source resolves the chain, embedded links included. If the real CSCA is itself only imported, after more than 63 decoys from the same source, `CHAIN_BUDGET` remains possible: this is the expected behavior in the face of a hostile import, without an additional cap per search.
  - **V35, unknown State code or DS from another country, without a chain** (complements V25, D40): when no chain completes and the known-country rule does not apply, a DG1 issuing State unknown to ICAO 9303-3 (neither an alpha-3 country, nor an ICAO-specific code, nor an organization) gives `CERTIFICATE_CHAIN` `FAILED` with `CheckDetail.Error("UNKNOWN_STATE_CODE")`, including when the DS cannot be found; a DS whose country (C attribute, absence included) differs from that of DG1 gives `FAILED` with `CheckDetail.CountryMismatch(null, pays du DS, État)`. The SOD signature remains “not available” there. “Unknown issuer” is reserved for real countries missing from the store and for organizations (`UNO`, `EUE`…), whose DS carries a code (`UN`, `EU`) with no alpha-3 equivalent.
  - **V40, signed attributes**: the EF.SOD `SignerInfo` must carry signed attributes, including `messageDigest` and a `contentType` equal to the encapsulated content type (`id-icao-ldsSecurityObject`), as required by ICAO 9303-10; otherwise the SOD is malformed (`SOD_MALFORMED` on the four PA lines). Same rule for EF.CardSecurity (`id-SecurityObject`), verified like the SOD: `VERIFY_CHIP-CAM-CARD_SECURITY_MALFORMED`.
- **Rationale**: verifying a hostile document must remain bounded in time, the content of an import must never degrade the verdict of a document that the embedded store authenticates, and a forger must not be able to choose the least unfavorable message.
- **Limitations**: the DG14 and DG15 keys are rebuilt by JMRTD when the file is read, before the check; their rebuild cost (validation by the provider) is not bounded by Sceau, only the cryptographic computation that follows is.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.2 (rejected algorithms: keys beyond the upper bounds are added, for all keys and no longer just AA; chain search order by source); §5.3 and §6.1 step 5 (“Unknown issuer” excluded for a State code unknown to ICAO or a DS from another country; SOD without signed attributes malformed).

## D43. Downgrade and CA announced without a key: revision of D40 (security audit of 2026-10-07: V32, V33)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: D40 only detects a protocol downgrade, and an announced but unusable CA, based on DG14 and its `id-PK-*` keys. The audit of 2026-10-07 identifies two cases where a clone without the chip's private key still gets “Valid signature, chip not verified” instead of “Failed”.
- **Decision**:
  - **V33, CA announced without a key** (complements V19): a DG14 that contains a `ChipAuthenticationInfo` (OID `id-CA-*`, prefix `0.4.0.127.0.7.2.2.3.`) without any `ChipAuthenticationPublicKeyInfo` (`id-PK-*`) gives `CHIP_AUTHENTICATION` `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM` (detail: the `id-CA-*` OID), hence “Failed”, without sending anything to the chip. `NOT_AVAILABLE` remains reserved for a DG14 that announces neither a CA key nor a CA protocol. With PACE-CAM, CA via DG14 is not performed (D21): the rule does not affect the CNIe;
  - **V32, EF.CardSecurity read after any PACE** (revises D40, V20): downgrade detection relied only on DG14; a clone of a document without DG14 or DG15, whose CA key is only in EF.CardSecurity, answered in PACE-GM after removing CAM from EF.CardAccess and got “Valid signature, chip not verified”. Now, after any successful PACE (GM, IM or CAM), EF.CardSecurity is read at the MF under PACE secure messaging, before the applet is selected (`Chip.readOptionalSecuredMasterFile`, `FileSizeLimits` bounds); it is never read under BAC. The PACE-CAM path (D21, V17) is unchanged and remains the only one to verify this file when CAM was performed. Without CAM, the file is verified like the SOD (`PassiveAuthenticator.verifyCardSecurity`, existing API) and `ProtocolDowngrade` adds it to DG14: if neither CA via DG14 nor AA succeeded and the CA line is `NOT_AVAILABLE`, PACE-CAM announced → `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-DOWNGRADE-CAM`), file present but invalid (signature, chain, country, structure) → `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-DOWNGRADE-CARD_SECURITY`), CA key or protocol announced → `FAILED` (`VERIFY_CHIP-DOWNGRADE-CA`). The content is only used to harden the CA line: content whose DS or CSCA cannot be found is taken as is, and its CSCA does not have to be that of the SOD (the V17 constraint specific to the CAM path), since a clone has nothing to gain from presenting a file rather than none. **Limitations**: a clone that does not serve EF.CardSecurity (file absent) escapes the rule, as it already escaped the DG14 rule by withholding a DG14 absent from the SOD; only the presence of DG14 or DG15 in the signed SOD, or reading in PACE-CAM, closes this case. **Accepted risk**: an authentic document read in PACE-GM whose EF.CardSecurity announces PACE-CAM or a CA key, without CA via DG14 or AA (for example after a transient PACE-CAM failure followed by PACE-GM), gets “Failed”; reading the document again is enough. **To be validated on a device**: reading EF.CardSecurity is now attempted after PACE-GM on documents that do not have it (a `6A82` under secure messaging must not close the session);
- **Rationale**: as for D40, a verdict must never become more favorable because a signed element announces a verification that the application could not perform.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §6.1 step 6 (a DG14 that announces CA without a usable key is an unsupported algorithm, not an absent step); §6.1 step 2 (EF.CardSecurity read after any PACE, compared with the channel performed).

## D44. Import into the store: reading bounded in duration, Master List signer anchored (revision of D22)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: security audit of 2026-10-07. V38: reading the file chosen through `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT` was bounded in size (20 MB, D22) but not in duration; a hostile `DocumentsProvider` that returns a pipe without ever delivering a byte blocked a `Dispatchers.IO` thread, since cancelling the coroutine does not interrupt the I/O, and the Import button stayed inert until the user left the screen. V34: an imported Master List was verified only with the signer it carries, without anchoring or usage check; a crafted file whose preview shows “signer: CSCA XY” turned each of its certificates into an anchor, and a complete forgery got “Authentic” with only the “imported” mention.
- **Decision**:
  - **Reading bounded to 30 s** (V38): `readImportedFile` (`app/.../ui/trust/TrustFormatting.kt`) opens and reads the file in a separate task, which the screen does not wait for beyond `IMPORT_READ_TIMEOUT_MILLIS`; on abandonment (timeout or screen left), the stream is closed, which unblocks `read` on Android, and `busy` goes back to false. Dedicated message `trust_import_read_timeout` (45 languages). Importing a standalone certificate goes through the same path.
  - **Signer anchored or flagged** (V34, option recommended by the audit): the signer of an imported Master List is chained, if possible, to an already known CSCA, embedded (ANTS, national publication, embedded Master List) or imported on its own (`MasterListParser.isSignerAnchored`, `TrustStores.isMasterListAnchored`); the CSCAs of another imported list and those of the list itself do not count, so that no list anchors itself on itself or on another unverified list. If not anchored, the list remains importable, but the preview shows a warning and “Import” is only enabled after a separate checkbox; its certificates take the new source `IMPORTED_MASTER_LIST_UNANCHORED`, lowest in priority, shown as “Imported list, unverified origin” in the store and in the result detail, and counted as an import for the verdict mention. Anchoring is computed at preview time and at load time on the same set of certificates.
  - **Extended key usage** (V34): if present, the signer's EKU must contain `id-icao-cscaMasterListSigningKey` (2.23.136.1.1.3) or `anyExtendedKeyUsage`, otherwise `BAD_SIGNER_USAGE`, for the embedded list as well as for imports. It is not required: some real Master Lists do not carry it. The BSI list's signer does.
- **Rationale**: 30 s leaves several orders of magnitude of margin to read 20 MB, even from a remote provider on a slow device; beyond that, the user keeps control of the screen. A thread still blocked despite the closing is not waited for: it prevents neither a new import nor leaving the screen. Refusing every non-anchored list would have forbidden legitimate lists (ICAO, authorities whose CSCA is missing from the store); the explicit warning and the separate confirmation make an import “without knowing it” impossible, and the distinct source keeps track of this choice all the way to the verdict.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §5.4 clarified (reading of the imported file bounded in size and duration; preview of a Master List with a non-anchored signer accompanied by a warning and a separate confirmation); §7.1 (additional source `IMPORTED_MASTER_LIST_UNANCHORED`).

## D46. Unnecessary AndroidX components removed from the merged manifest (revision of D7, security audit of 2026-10-07: V39)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: as for the D7 permission, AndroidX dependencies add to the merged manifest components that Sceau does not use. `androidx.profileinstaller` (transitive from Compose) declares `ProfileInstallReceiver` there, **exported** (protected by the `android.permission.DUMP` permission, actions `INSTALL_PROFILE`, `SKIP_FILE`, `SAVE_PROFILE`, `BENCHMARK_OPERATION`): only `adb shell` and the system can reach it, but it is the only exported component besides `MainActivity`, whereas SPEC §8 aims for a zero attack surface. `androidx.startup.InitializationProvider` additionally carries the `ProfileInstallerInitializer` and `EmojiCompatInitializer` (emoji2) initializers, run at every startup. No Sceau class calls `ProfileInstaller`, `EmojiCompat` or `ProcessLifecycleOwner`, and Sceau provides no `baseline-prof.txt`.
- **Decision**: in `app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`, `tools:node="remove"` on the `androidx.profileinstaller.ProfileInstallReceiver` receiver and on the `ProfileInstallerInitializer` and `EmojiCompatInitializer` `meta-data` of `InitializationProvider`. `ProcessLifecycleInitializer` (lifecycle-process, on which AndroidX libraries rely) is kept, and therefore so is the provider (not exported). These two elements carry `tools:ignore="MissingClass"`: `androidx.startup` and `androidx.profileinstaller` are only transitive runtime dependencies, absent from the compile classpath that lint consults.
- **Rationale**: the release APK now declares only one exported component, `MainActivity` (`aapt2 dump xmltree --file AndroidManifest.xml app-universal-release-unsigned.apk`). Without its initializer, emoji2 no longer looks for a downloadable font provider at startup; Compose then displays emoji with the system fonts, which affects no Sceau text.
- **Consequence**: the baseline ART profile merged from the libraries (`assets/dexopt/baseline.prof`, brought by Compose) remains in the APK, but is no longer installed by profileinstaller on the first launch of an installation outside a store (F-Droid, GitHub release): ahead-of-time compilation of the Compose code happens progressively through use by ART, at the cost of a slightly slower first startup. The profile can still serve installations that deliver it themselves (`.dm` file).
- **To be rechecked** at each AndroidX upgrade: that no new exported component or initializer appears in the release merged manifest, and that `ProcessLifecycleInitializer` is still the only required initializer.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none; applies §8 (zero surface) and extends D7 to components other than permissions.

## D47. Anchor RSA keys checked on use, not when the store is loaded (complements D16)

- **Date**: 2026-10-07.
- **Context**: loading the store (D16) took 38 s (release APK) to 45 s (debug APK) on a Fairphone 3, for 47 to 68 s of computation. Preloading at startup usually hid it, but after the process died in the background, Android reopens the Trust store screen directly, which stayed on “Loading the trust store…” the whole time. Cause, measured by stack sampling on the JVM: `MasterListParser.toX509OrNull` decoded the public key of each of the roughly 600 certificates to discard unreadable keys; for each RSA key (about 430, most of them 4,096 bits), BouncyCastle (`RSAKeyParameters.validate`) then runs a Miller-Rabin test proving that the modulus is not prime. BouncyCastle's cache of validated moduli does not survive the process: each cold start paid the full cost.
- **Decision**: for an `rsaEncryption` key, `toX509OrNull` now only reads its structure (ASN.1 `RSAPublicKey`: positive odd modulus, positive exponent), without building the key object. Other keys (EC, RSASSA-PSS…) are decoded as before. BouncyCastle's check is neither disabled nor bypassed: it is deferred to the moment an anchor's key is used to verify a signature (chain search, chaining of the signer of an imported Master List, D44), which recreates the key and checks it fully. A failure at that moment counts as a rejection.
- **Rationale**: an anchor's trust comes from its provenance (embedded file, verified Master List and, for imports, the user's consent), not from the form of its key; the author of a booby-trapped list chooses the keys they put in it anyway. Keys coming from the chip (DS, Chip Authentication, Active Authentication) do not go through this path and are still checked as soon as they are decoded.
- **Verification**: `PrimeModulusAnchorTest`: a CSCA with a prime modulus (private key trivial to compute) is accepted when a Master List is loaded (the test fails without the fix), then the chain of a DS it signed, a mathematically valid signature verified by a control outside BouncyCastle, is refused (`FAILED` or `UNSUPPORTED_ALGORITHM`, never `OK`). `MasterListTest` (unreadable key, unknown algorithm) unchanged. On a Fairphone 3, debug APK: store loaded in under 10 s after a cold start (18 s of computation instead of 68), and in about 3 s after process death on the Trust store screen.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none.

- **Decision**: the first public release is **1.0.0**: `versionName = "1.0.0"`, `versionCode = 1`, annotated tag `v1.0.0`, assets `sceau-1.0.0-<abi>.apk` and `sceau-1.0.0-universal.apk`. ## D54. v2 and v3 signing only, F-Droid recipe without `scandelete`

- **Date**: 2026-10-12.
- **Context**: `fdroid build` of 1.0.0 rebuilds a universal APK identical byte for byte to ours, but copying the signature fails (“APK integrity check failed. CHUNKED_SHA512 digest mismatch”). Test with a throwaway key on the same unsigned APK: signed with v1 + v2 + v3 by apksigner 37, `apksigcopier compare` fails (with or without `--alignment-preserved`); signed with v2 + v3, it succeeds. Furthermore, fdroidserver (2.4.5 as well as master) rejects the recipe's two `scandelete` paths (`openjpeg/tools`, `openjpeg/src/bin/wx`): its scanner finds nothing to remove there, and an unnecessary path is an error.
- **Decision**: the APKs are signed with **v2 and v3 only** (`--v1-signing-enabled false`), like Roue Libre; `apksigcopier compare` becomes a mandatory check of each APK before publication (`docs/release.md` §4). The assets of the `v1.0.0` release, published half an hour earlier with a v1 signature, were replaced by the same APKs signed again; the tag and the commit did not move. The recipe no longer has `scandelete`, and gains `Changelog` (releases page). The local settings that allow `fdroid build` are described in `docs/fdroid/README.md`.
- **Rationale**: minSdk 26 does not require v1, and the reproducibility verified by F-Droid is a precondition for publishing the APK signed by the author. Replacing half-hour-old assets avoids a 1.0.1 version with no code change; only the files' fingerprint changes, not their content or the certificate.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none.

## D55. English as the default language

- **Date**: 2026-10-12.
- **Context**: the French strings occupied `res/values/`, English `values-en/`. Android falls back to `res/values/` when the phone's language has no translation: a user whose language is not among the 45 offered (D27), for example Swahili, Persian or Indonesian, saw the interface in French.
- **Decision**: English becomes the default language. The English strings move to `res/values/strings_<screen>.xml` (moved from `values-en/`), the French ones to a new `values-fr/` (moved from `values/`). `values-en/` disappears: its last file, `strings.xml`, only contained a comment. The `translatable="false"` strings (repository and donation URLs, library names, `MRZ`, identifiers DG1…EF.CardSecurity) and `app_name` remain in `values/` only. The seven French plurals gain a `many` category identical to `other`, as in es, it and pt (D27): the `MissingQuantity` lint check, which does not check the default folder, requires it for `values-fr/`. `res/xml/locales_config.xml` is unchanged: it already offers `fr` and `en`, English being served by `values/`. No code depends on the default language; tests that check a French text already set their locale (`@Config(qualifiers = "fr…")`), and the F-Droid screenshots are still produced in fr-FR and en-US (D39).
- **Rationale**: Sceau targets an international audience (trust store covering 110 countries, F-Droid); English is the most widely understood fallback language, and the one F-Droid reviewers expect. French speakers see no difference: a phone set to French, whatever the region (`fr-FR`, `fr-BE`, `fr-CA`…), gets `values-fr/`.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: §2 (“Interface in French”) and error messages “in French” on the reading screen; SPEC updated.

## D56. F-Droid recipe built with JDK 21 alone (revises D49)

- **Date**: 2026-10-13.
- **Context**: in the fdroiddata merge request, the reviewer asked why the recipe installs two JDKs (17 from bookworm, 25 from trixie) and noted that the JDK version generally does not affect reproducibility. D49 and `docs/reproducible-builds.md` assumed that the daemon JDK, which runs R8, and the toolchain JDK were part of the reproducible environment, without having tested it.
- **Decision**: the recipe drops `sudo:` and the `auto-download=false` line. It removes `gradle/gradle-daemon-jvm.properties` with `rm:`, so the daemon runs on the server's default JDK 21, and replaces `jvmToolchain(17)` with `jvmToolchain(21)` in the three modules in `prebuild:`. The foojay plugin removal stays. The repository keeps JDK 25 for the daemon and 17 for the toolchains; the JDK rows leave the table of versions to pin.
- **Rationale**: measured, not assumed. 1.0.0 built in clean clones with daemon/toolchain JDKs 25/17, 21/17, 17/17 and 21/21 (auto-download and auto-detection off, only the listed JDKs visible), then with the exact recipe steps under JDK 21: the five universal APKs are identical byte for byte, and `apksigcopier compare` matches each one against the signed release APK; a per-ABI APK is correctly reported as different. R8 and D8 output does not depend on the JVM that runs them. The recipe becomes shorter and installs nothing.
- **Deviation from the SPEC**: none.
